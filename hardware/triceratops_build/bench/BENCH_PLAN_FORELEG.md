## Test article and loads

The robot is the 12S, twelve-axis build with ten X4 drives and two X6 drives. The foreleg has X4 roll, X4 pitch and an X6 elbow through a nominal 1:1 pushrod. Fit the D19 half-capsule rocker to the passive **pitch** ankle. The [Option C parts list](../design/PARTS_60CM.md), [decisions D2, D4, D9, D15 and D19](../DECISION_LOG.md), and [capsule model](../sim/generated/trike60_C_closed_capsule.xml) define the article. The compute selection is open under D3. The bench baseline is Teensy/classic CAN.

| Foreleg axis | Specified drive type | Model peak / thermal reference, joint N·m | Estimated walking peak / held load, drive-output N·m |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Shoulder roll | RMD-X4-P36-36-E-L | 21.0 / 7.5 | 9.88 / 4.40 |
| Shoulder pitch | RMD-X4-P36-36-E-L | 21.0 / 7.5 | 9.48 / 3.72 |
| Elbow flexion | RMD-X6-P20-60-E | 37.8 / 10.8 | 18.05 / 9.18 |

The last column is a sizing estimate from `check()` in [size_budget.py](../budget/size_budget.py), with Option C at 12.6938 kg. It includes 0.90 transmission efficiency at the elbow. The [closed mass ledger](../sim/generated/mass_ledger_trike60_C_closed_capsule.json) puts 55.32% of the static load on the forefeet, or about **34.4 N per forefoot** for an evenly loaded level stance. Use the dynamic loads as well when sizing the fixture. In the [capsule demo study](../rl/README.md), the 0.15 m/s segment produced a 3.26-body-weight peak front-foot load (about 406 N), a 3.34-body-weight peak front-elbow reaction (about 416 N), and 39.1 N·m of peak front-pitch bending. Bearing reactions and bending are structural quantities. Keep them separate from actuator output torque. Dynamic touchdown forces, the held-paw pose and the controlled sit need their own traces. Save the model revision, gait/checkpoint, command, floor, seed, side and timestamps with each selected trace. Include both forelegs when selecting the more demanding trace for each axis.

The BOM prices two X4s and one X6 at **$966 for drives alone** before freight, duty or tax. The whole robot’s $2,229 bench line already includes its supply, torque rig, USB-CAN adapter, camera and gantry. Confirm which items are available and separately quote the reaction load, reference instruments, enclosure, any extra bus channels and logging hardware. Size the reaction drive, lever, linkage and restraints for the actual load and direction. The foreleg test does not need an X8 or four installed foot load cells. ## Commissioning the Teensy/CAN leg

1. **Identify before applying power.** Record drive labels, serials or traceable labels where none are exposed, firmware, bus/node IDs, physical joints and cable labels. Read the shipped data sheets. The US X6 listing shows 0.78 kg and 50 N·m peak under an X6-60 heading; the model uses the 0.82 kg X6-60. Recompute B1 and B3 for a substitution. The exact X4 data sheet specifies 24 V and 83 rpm rated. Obtain written limits for continuous input, transients and braking on the shipped X4 before any 12S test. A fully charged standard 12S LiPo is 50.4 V; the project also discusses a 49.2 V charge ceiling (4.10 V per cell). Record the pack and charger settings and the maximum actually allowed on the robot. Until the operating and transient limits are known, use documented rated conditions for standalone drive checks and leave the 12S rows open. [X4 data sheet](https://cdn.sanity.io/files/7rgy42zg/production/3c195724e1cf66a99c947f146565051eec2bfc4a.pdf) · [US X6 listing](https://myactuator.us/products/rmd-x-series/rmd-x6-p20-60)
2. **Record the configuration before motion.** One versioned manifest records joint-to-bus mapping, identities and firmware, command/reply units, encoder side, sign and zero, gear ratio, limits on position/speed/current/torque, gains, command expiry, reset procedure and supply limits with their sources. Mark estimates and unknowns. Lock out motion for a duplicate ID, wrong drive/configuration, missing critical limit, stale command or missing required stop input. Power-cycle and verify zero, direction, units and bounds against an external angle mark and a calibrated lever. Start at limited current with each drive unloaded and the stop chain already wired. Before enabling, check wiring continuity, isolation, bus termination and configured bit rate. Decode a known positive and negative reading for each signal, including byte order, scaling and saturation. Record the manifest and calibration IDs against each passed combination. Recheck affected rows after a swap or change to firmware, gains, mapping, stop settings or linkage. This adapts [PLEM’s commissioning practice](https://github.com/WIM-Corporation/PLEM/blob/a9a5d12ef1f63cfeaef945502d6648567186ab26/README.md#L91-L107) to CAN.
3. **Protect and instrument the rig.** Guard the sweep and reaction lever. Fit a current-limited supply, precharge, fuse, clamp, wired pendant/mushroom, watchdog and contactor. Prove cut and manual reset unloaded. For supported load tests use a shoulder fixture and independent catch/restraint that takes the entire load after loss of all power. For the sit test allow vertical travel to a representative belly skid/stop and instrument skid seating separately from catch engagement. A fixed shoulder or a taut catch that carries the test load invalidates a sit trial. Use a separately rated energy sink for powered braking; verify that the bench supply can absorb regeneration or isolate it as designed.
4. **Log synchronized raw data.** Use an independent recorder with a common clock or measured offset/drift. Record tick, command sequence, CAN transmit/receive time, feedback sample time or bounded sample age, actual position/speed/current, physical torque or calibrated onset sensor, bus voltage and signed current, temperatures and faults. Record stop request, command expiry, watchdog, relay, skid, catch and contactor states against the same clock. Record sensor units, sign convention, sampling rate, uncertainty, drop counters, clock calibration and thermal sensor location. Use a scope for bus transients and contact transitions; a slow software log cannot establish their peaks. Preserve raw traces, manifest, firmware and calibration, then compute summaries with the run ID and revision.

### Run record

One commissioning record identifies the physical leg, all three drives, CAN firmware revision, calibration files, supply/charge limits, cover revision, chosen foot, ambient conditions, test operator and date. Keep one monotonically timed sample stream and one event stream per run. Preserve bus and joint identity on every row. A practical minimum export is shown below. A blank cell means unavailable and the run metadata states why.

```text
samples: t_us,seq,bus,node,joint,tick_us,tx_us,rx_us,feedback_sample_us,cmd_pos_rad,actual_pos_rad,actual_speed_rad_s,current_A,torque_measured_Nm,bus_V,bus_signed_A,case_C,drive_temp_C,status,drop_count
events: t_us,source,event,state,seq,details
```

For B3, collect the drive’s temperature-field definition and separate winding measurement or validated proxy. For B5/B8, attach externally time-aligned torque-onset, contactor, catch and support channels. Note the logger’s clock wrap, reset handling, time synchronization residual and dropped samples. Reconstruct one trace from a saved run before treating the logging path as commissioned.

## Measurements and release rules

Keep a result row for each axis, condition and repetition. Record the predeclared target, measurement, uncertainty, trace ID and verdict separately. A missing prerequisite, unobserved endpoint or measurement unable to distinguish pass from fail leaves that row open. Timing estimates and screen captures cannot clear a hardware gate.

| ID | Procedure | Acceptance, and what to do with the result |
|---|---|---|
| **B1 — Torque and tracking** | Measure output torque at 10 rpm on the calibrated reaction rig, three repeats per drive and permitted voltage. On the assembled elbow also measure joint torque through the linkage. For stall, use only the duration, current and temperature allowed by the shipped data sheet, with cooldown. Replay the chosen Option C load/speed traces. | For the budget walk, measured peak × 0.70 must cover the last column’s walking peak for each drive; also cover the chosen trajectory’s joint demand at its concurrent speed. Retain the incoming-drive check of ≥90% of the shipped variant’s documented peak at its specified test point, and ≥90% of documented stall where a maker test is permissible. Log an unspecified stall figure as unqualified. Update the model to measured limits if lower. |
| **B2 — Shipped variant** | Photograph the label, read firmware and bus IDs, weigh each drive; compare zero after restart, direction and torque/current scaling against the manifest. Confirm voltage limits in writing and exact X6 SKU/firmware from the vendor. | Release only the identified configuration and voltage range. A different X6 requires updated inertia, torque envelope and heat duty before a foreleg verdict. |
| **B3 — Thermal duty** | With shell, cooling and wiring as designed, replay 20 min of the selected walk, two 2-min held-paw segments and 6 min standing. Run at 25 °C and 35 °C ambient. Repeat the planned 2-min trot segment separately and record cooldown. Reproduce torque **and** speed. Record drive-reported temperature, what physical temperature the register represents, and case temperature independently. | At 35 °C meet the inherited winding target ≤90 °C and any lower validated component limit with no thermal derating or fault. Where winding temperature is unavailable, use an independently validated proxy with uncertainty or keep the winding criterion open. Record airflow and peak temperature by axis. Establish a revised duty cycle or cooling design and repeat after a failure. |
| **B4 — X4 speed and bus voltage** | With the variant/voltage envelope cleared under B2, step through bench supply setpoints including the 42 V design low, 49 V intermediate and approved upper pack condition. Record unloaded speed, and track the actual Option C commanded speed under its simultaneous swing torque. Repeat a 30-min run at the planned upper operating condition within the verified range. | Use the budget’s 15% headroom convention for the selected swing profile: required speed ≤0.85 × measured available speed **under that load**. Verify the configured 83 rpm cap and record tracking error, temperature and faults. If the documented supply range, loaded speed or duty fails, rework power, drive or target speed and rerun the walk. |
| **B5 — End-to-end timing** | Use the four-bus, 3-drives-per-leg traffic pattern. Test X4 and X6 at 500 Hz and 1 kHz under idle and representative loaded compute on the Teensy path. For a Jetson/USB-CAN comparison use one real interface per concurrent bus. See the detailed timing protocol below. | At the chosen operating rate, bus load ≤80% and zero lost frames in the 30-min soaks. Apply D9’s 10 ms threshold using the appropriate feedback baseline below. Re-run the walk at measured delay and feedback age; report tail outliers separately. |
| **B6 — Elbow transmission** | Measure play at ±2 N·m reversal and stiffness up to the selected elbow load, on both loading and unloading. Sweep the entire −47° to +36° elbow range for interference, transmission angle and actual ratio. Inspect crank/axle clearance, fasteners, rod-end articulation, pull and buckling capacity against measured force. | Preserve the fixture’s play ≤1.0°, stiffness ≥400 N·m/rad and transmission angle ≥40° screening targets. Check the physical assembly under the required load in both directions. Record actual load covered, elastic deflection, clearance and any permanent set. Use measured inertia/stiffness for a 60 cm mode estimate before claiming any resonant frequency. |
| **B7 — Regenerative braking** | Commit a timestamped twelve-axis hard-sit trajectory and the resulting peak power, pulse duration and energy before high-energy tests. Estimate net surplus at the bus after simultaneous motoring/auxiliary loads. With pack charging blocked as designed, instrument one-leg deceleration then a controlled equivalent pulse from a separate, rated source. Integrate measured signed DC power and clamp dissipation. | Preserve the proposed 52.5 V clamp / ≤54 V bus criterion **only if** all shipped parts and the verified X4 transient envelope permit it. Apply the lower verified component limit, including overshoot. The injected waveform must cover both target energy and peak power; measure resistor temperature after ten representative pulses at the declared spacing, within its published pulse-energy, repetition and temperature limits. A one-leg stop or a watts-only estimate leaves the twelve-axis row open. |
| **B8 — Stops and fault response** | With the leg supported as described above, exercise radio press/lost link, wired mushroom, pendant, stale host commands, bus interruption and failed computer/MCU heartbeat. Log physical load path and opening of the contactor. For each controlled-stop input test 20 trials, with a measured and recorded relay delay (design setting 0.5–1.5 s). Verify restart requires an intentional reset. | For controllable stops the leg seats on the representative skid ≥0.2 s before power drops in **20/20** trials per input. For a failed controller or severed drive bus, record whether a path to command the sit remains, actual isolation time, displacement and catch load; a catch arrest or uncontrolled drop is a failed sit. Prove independent isolation on loss of control and escalate any unsupported load case for design resolution before walking. A foreleg result cannot certify a free-standing quadruped or a public demo. |
| **B9 — Capsule sole** | Fit the physical rubber half-capsule (88 mm long, 24 mm radius, 40 mm straight section) on the passive pitch hinge. Use an external force plate and marked/high-speed views of contact migration, rolling and sliding on planned floor samples. Replay representative stance and lift, including lateral transfer and repeated forefoot touchdowns. Inspect rubber, retention and ankle-stop clearance under load. | Compare net sliding at actual contact with the registered C5c criterion ≤10 mm from 0.10 s after touchdown until lift-off. Track rolling separately. Record force, angle, slide and touchdown counts with measurement uncertainty. Where the fixture cannot reproduce that transfer, keep the gait criterion open for the supported chassis trial. Verify clearance and retention through travel; rework or restrict the surface if the trial fails. [Foot study and measure](../sim/PREREG_FEET60.md) |

### B5 timing protocol and the feedback baseline

Use the [shared bench sheet](../../trex_form_study/stage2/jetson75/BENCH_SHEET.md), sections 1, 3, 4.1 and 5, for M1 coarse/fine and the independently calibrated M2 physical onset check. Keep its shared T. rex comparison rows and five soaks identified separately. For the Triceratops, use 1,000 commands per row, the 300-command M1 fine check on the verdict row, and 30-minute soaks for each candidate path/rate under load. Emulate the absent drives’ command **and reply** traffic on all four buses, mark it as emulated, and repeat with twelve physical drives before the free-standing robot is released. Log achieved rate, utilization, missed deadlines, late/lost/retried frames and missing replies by bus. Use the worst axis/load condition on the selected path. Record p50/p99/max tick-to-torque `T`, feedback age at use `S`, and controller compute time. If sensing and actuator clocks differ, document synchronization or conservative bounds. Include controller compute once in the end-to-end value when using the deployed loop.

The T. rex sheet rounds `p99(T) + max(0, p99(S) − 2 ms)` up to a 2 ms step because its simulation already contains 2 ms of sensor age. Keep that reading and its 10 ms comparison in the historical B5 column for both robots, so previous work can be compared. The [Triceratops environment](../rl/trike_env.py) delays leg targets through a 1 ms FIFO and samples the current simulated state for its observations. Record the command delay and feedback age separately for its replay. For this revision’s additional Triceratops check, until sensor age is represented explicitly, use `p99(T) + p99(S)` as a conservative **screening sum** and round up to a whole 1 ms physics step for the D9 comparison. This sum of marginal percentiles is not a measured joint percentile. Also compute the p99 of paired `T + S` on synchronized samples and report the observed tail. If the measured envelope exceeds 10 ms, rerun the controller at that envelope and revisit the selected rate/path before a four-leg order. Explicit sensor-delay replay adds the measured age once and uses the measured command delay separately.

### Order of work and limits of a pass

1. Complete B2, the physical stop-chain checkout and calibration. Run B1/B4/B5 on restrained drives at their approved supply and limits before fitting the linkage. Complete B6 before replaying high loads through it.
2. Run B3 with the actual cover, then compare against an uncovered run at matched ambient, airflow settings, voltage, speed and load. Record the cover mass, vent free area and duct routing. Use the same rig and an X4 for an additional hind-knee duty replay with 1:1 flexion and measured efficiency. The sizing estimate is 7.51 N·m held at the drive, close to the 7.50 N·m thermal reference. Include the intended held-paw and ramp duty. In the selected capsule-foot terrain replay, right hind-knee RMS torque averaged 1.12 × its continuous reference across eight seeds on each of the 3° and 5° grades. Replay both profiles, and include the fore-pitch limit events and front-foot impacts in the foreleg test. [Terrain results and conditions](../review60/README.md) A front X6 pass cannot clear the hind X4, and different cooling in a hind cover needs its own confirmation. [Sizing findings F12–F16](../DECISION_LOG.md)
3. Run B7/B8 with independent restraint and B9 on the intended floor material. Rerun the 60 cm simulation with the shipped variant, measured torque/speed envelope, delay, heat, play, stiffness and sole behavior. Review open and failed rows before ordering the remaining legs. Repeat stop/regen/contact and timing tests on the caught assembled chassis before free walking.

## Optional EtherCAT comparison (D7 remains open)

The practices above work on CAN without installing PLEM. Upstream PLEM requires a drive-operated holding brake; the specified leg drives are brakeless. Its supported host is the AGX Orin Developer Kit, its one-bus maximum is eight axes, and WIM documents a two-bus system as hardware-unverified. Its published timing is a loop benchmark on two Panasonic axes. [Brake and mode requirements](https://github.com/WIM-Corporation/PLEM/blob/a9a5d12ef1f63cfeaef945502d6648567186ab26/docs/getting-started.md#L641-L658) · [Host and two-bus support](https://github.com/WIM-Corporation/PLEM/blob/a9a5d12ef1f63cfeaef945502d6648567186ab26/docs/install.md) · [Timing evidence](https://github.com/WIM-Corporation/PLEM/blob/a9a5d12ef1f63cfeaef945502d6648567186ab26/docs/benchmark/README.md)

For a paper evaluation, match the shipped part/firmware to [MyActuator’s ESI](https://www.myactuator.com/downloads-ethercatesi), unit conventions, permissible cycle/sync settings, and drive response to communication loss. A project-hardware pilot requires owner approval (D7), a WIM-supported path for these exact brakeless drives and verified fault behavior. If those prerequisites are met, use the independent rig for the two-drive test, then compare the same three-axis load on CAN and EtherCAT with B5 and B8. Until then, upstream motion examples are applicable to supported braked hardware only. Keep any future whole-robot migration conditional on a measured twelve-axis result, stop response and a fresh compute/power/mass budget.

# The foreleg comparison (D22): 128 against 160 mm under the RL policy. Report

26 Nov 2026. **Draft, under independent review.** **Simulation only.** Nothing here was measured on hardware. Rules: `PREREG_FORELEG_D22.md` (D22, D23,
D24). Every figure: `REPORT_FORELEG_D22_APPENDIX.md`, generated by `d22_appendix.py` from the stored tool output.
Run history: `runs/trike60-d22_LOG.md`.

## The answer

**The pre-registered judge chooses 128 mm.** 128 mm is the only length whose four policies all pass both
walking rules:
- F1: steady flat walking at 0.25 m/s with no falls;
- F2: every drive's RMS torque under its continuous rating.

160 mm failed F2 on three of its four seeds, each time on a different drive: 1.014, 1.013 and 1.094 of rating.
Under D24's order a length that fails F1 or F2 on any seed loses when the other passes on all four. So the
foot-load, fall and heat tests on the four-seed means were never reached.

Had they been applied, they would also have kept 128 mm:
- **Loads:** 160 mm's mean front-foot load was 117.6 N against 128 mm's 121.6 N, a ratio of 0.967. The rule
  needed 0.85 or less.
- **Heat:** 160 mm's mean heat was worse, both overall and on the front drives.

This is a simulation result about this setup. It is an input to the owner's decision under D23 ("128 mm unless
beaten"), not the decision itself.

**What it does not say.** "128 mm chosen" means 160 mm did not walk within the drives' ratings on every seed. It
does not mean 128 mm is better in every respect.

Both lengths run their drives close to the continuous rating:
- In the judged case, 128 mm's worst drive reaches 0.83-0.95 of rating.
- In two cases that are reported but not judged, one 128 mm seed reaches 1.00 and 1.005: the sweep's nominal point,
  and the gait report's nominal model with noise off.

All eight policies walk straight only. PR #697's case for 160 mm came from turning and pivoting loads, which these
runs do not test.

Eight runs: four seeds (0-3) for each of the two forelegs, `sim/generated/trike60_C_closed_capsule_fore128.xml`
(12.751 kg) and `..._fore160.xml` (12.808 kg). The two models are PR #697's repackaged forelegs with length-scaled
masses (A-D22a).

The setup was run 3's, unchanged apart from the model:
- `configs/run3_terrain.json`;
- 10,000,000 steps, 8 envs, the same PPO settings;

Each run had one session, from step 0, completed at 10,002,432 steps, with a finite final pair. Training took
3.57-5.13 h at 542-779 env steps/s.

The judged pair is each run's final pair. Its sha256 is in the run log and in each tool's provenance.

Measurement used the run commit's tools from a `git archive` of f84a6919, through `TRIKE_ENV_DIR`, on this machine:
- the gait report;
- the robustness sweep (70 points, 8 episodes each);
- `foot_loads.py`;
- the demo course (reported only).

## What this can and cannot conclude

- **It can say:** under run 3's training, with these two models and four seeds each, 160 mm's policies did not keep
  every drive under its rating on three of four seeds; 128 mm's did on all four.
- **On the F2 margins:** they are small. 160 mm failed by 1.3-9.4 %, and 128 mm's worst seed passed with 5.5 % to
  spare. With other seeds or other training settings either length could land on either side. The gate is strict by
  design (D24).
- **It cannot say anything about turning:** these policies walk straight. Turning loads and heat under the RL policy
  wait for D20.
- **The heat figures are model estimates:** the rating, the thermal model and the masses are estimates, the servo
  loops are idealised, and the policy observes the simulator's true velocity. The bench article measures the real
  drives' temperature and current.
- **Geometry is outside this study:** the moving pushrod, the dressed meshes' clearances and the shoulder
  crossmember (F19) are not part of it.

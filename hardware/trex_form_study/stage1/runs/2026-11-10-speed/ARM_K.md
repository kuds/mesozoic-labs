# Stage 1b arm K: how its bodies were built, and what a checkout needs

Arm K (`STAGE1B_PLAN.md` sections 2 and 3) runs the speed fit on the two 60 cm picks with round two's faster knee.
These notes record how the K bodies were built and checked, what a checkout needs before their fits, and what is
still open. The commands are in `run_arm_k.sh`. The git-ignored inputs are listed with their sha256 in
`arm_k_inputs.json`, and `arm_k_inputs.py` checks, copies and regenerates them.

## 1. The bodies

The refine case `narrow_knee_60` (`designlab/refine.py`) is `narrow_carpet_60` (the same ids, overrides, walks and
3 mm turn gap) with `knee_60`'s 15 % knee headroom (`headroom_by_group {"knee": 0.85}`). Each K body is named after
its pick with `-K` and carries the pick's round-two candidate id (`designlab/stage1/__init__.py`: `BODIES`,
`CANDIDATE_IDS`, `candidate_id`).

| | `60-N-T-10-K` | `60-N-H-10-K` |
|---|---|---|
| Model `xml_sha256` (`SHA256_PREFIX`, plan section 3) | `85d98c24…` | `e0d4c6b7…` |
| Drives changed from the pick | the knee only: X4-36-L to RobStride 06 | the knee only: X4-36-L to RobStride 06 |
| Round two's gates | all pass | clearance fails (`joint_range_margin`): section 4 |
| Stage 1 G1–G4 (decisions 11–20; G4 at the decision-16 start) | all pass | all pass |

The sizing, the carpet-lift pass, `prepare` and G1–G4 ran on the code that commit 4b704e4 holds (section 5), with
`run_arm_k.sh` on 3 processes.

## 2. The git-ignored inputs

A merge does not bring these files. `arm_k_inputs.json` lists all 60 with their sha256:

- `round2/refine/narrow_knee_60/models/60-N-T-10.{xml,json}` and `60-N-H-10.{xml,json}`: the case's round-two
  models. The plant reads them, and every K record key hashes them (`panels.key_context`).
- `<body>/refs/*.npz`: 10 per body. Their sha256 are in `refs.json`.
- `<body>/transitions/*.npz`: 18 per body. Their sha256 are in `transitions.json`.

The case's `review/mjcf/*.xml`, `models/narrow_search/` and `round2/refine/carpet_lift_models/` are not needed.

After the merge, run these in the main checkout, from `hardware/trex_form_study`:

```
bash stage1/runs/2026-11-10-speed/run_arm_k.sh copy-from ../../.claude/worktrees/speed-s2
bash stage1/runs/2026-11-10-speed/run_arm_k.sh check
```

`copy-from` checks each file's sha256 before and after it copies it. Without that worktree, `run_arm_k.sh run`
regenerates the files exactly and keeps every committed record as it is:

- The models are regenerated by the sizing's last call (about 3 min per body) and checked against the list.
- The arrays are regenerated in a scratch directory. Each is put in place only if it matches `refs.json` or
  `transitions.json`. The transitions take about 11 min per body on 3 processes.

Both paths were tested on 10 Nov, on a scratch copy of the study that started without these files:

- `copy-from` brought all 60 files.
- `run` then regenerated, each with its listed sha256: `60-N-H-10-K`'s removed model files, two `60-N-T-10-K`
  reference arrays (one removed, one damaged) and two `60-N-H-10-K` transition arrays.
- The models step also regenerated `60-N-T-10-K`'s removed XML and damaged ledger JSON.

Two other ways to get the files are wrong:

- **Letting the plant rebuild the models.** Without the case's models, the plant rebuilds them into
  `stage1/models/<body>/`. The XML is the same, but the model ledger JSON differs: it has no `evaluation` section and
  another path. Every K record key then differs, and `eval` refuses the checkout.
- **Re-running `refine --cases narrow_knee_60`.** This regenerates the models, but it also rewrites the committed
  candidate records. Each record holds its run time, so the records' hashes in the keys change with it.

## 3. Source hashes and Stage 1's records

Registering the K bodies edits five of the panels' modules (`__init__`, `plant`, `refs`, `control`, `panels`). That
alone changes the panels' and the tuner's source hashes. The build's commit message says the other branch's `tune.py`
edit has the same effect. It does not: main at c94202b changes none of the panels' or the tuner's modules, and keeps
both hashes.

| Sources (sha256, first 8) | Stage 1's records | 07a73f4 (Stage 1's code) | c94202b (main) | 4b704e4 (arm K's build) | This branch now |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Panels (`panels.PANEL_SOURCES_RULE`) | `7516b927` | `7516b927` | `7516b927` | `bad29edb` | `733f2b80` |
| Tuner (`tune.TUNER_SOURCES_RULE`) | `c8b112cf` (fits, G5) | `e7b37918` | `e7b37918` | `8491a17f` | `6a33d3f9` |

What follows from this:

- **Panels.** With the round's code, `eval` and `sweep` would read every Stage 1 panel record (128 / 128 / 184) and
  margin record (9 / 9 / 17) as made by other code, and would run them again over the committed ones.
- **Tuner.** Stage 1's fits and G5 records name `c8b112cf` (commit 8155886, 9 Nov). Later Stage 1 commits changed
  the tuner's modules: the faster tracker (decision 21), the PPO build and the panels. So `tune --resume` and `g5` on
  Stage 1's fits are refused with Stage 1's own final code too, and the K registration adds nothing there.
- **The speed fit is not affected.** It reads a Stage 1 G5 selection's gains (`speedfit.START_RULE`,
  `env.tuned_gains`). It checks the selection's G5 status and fixed gains, not the sources that fitted them.
- **Decision 6's rule holds in full.** No `eval`, `sweep`, `tune` or `g5` runs against `stage1/runs/2026-11-08` with
  the round's code. Stage 1's tables are regenerated only from its own commit (07a73f4 or earlier).
- **Why the hashes were not kept.** Keeping them would mean registering the K bodies outside the panels' and the
  tuner's modules. That redesign was not done, because main's speed fit reads `candidate_id` from the registry.

Nothing else about the probe's three bodies changes. These are identical: the model files, `plant.verify`, the plant
inputs in `panels.key_context`, `plant.describe`, the usable ranges, the dropped limits and the regenerated
references. Their committed panel and ledger keys recompute exactly with each record's own panel-sources hash
(`tests/test_stage1_bodies.py`).

## 4. `60-N-H-10-K` is limited by its body (open: decide before the fits start)

- **Round two's clearance gate fails** (`joint_range_margin`). Hip-pitch extension now stops at −0.509 rad (the pick:
  −0.951), where the upper-thigh shell meets the power-distribution board. The packaging search has no lever for that,
  so it stopped at +6 mm and never reached the turn gates:
  - the target turns clear 0.84 / 0.28 mm, where the case asks for 3 mm;
  - the 0.5 rad/s walking turn interferes (−3.59 mm).
- **Its fast references lack joint range.** It keeps 0.124 rad on the nominal walk, but only 0.070 rad on the brisk
  walk and 0.040 rad on the Froude 0.05 walk, at the hip pitch (G2's `reference_range_margins`). Stage 1 keeps
  0.10 rad on walks (`STAGE1_PLAN.md` decision 14). So even perfect tracking cannot meet every limit on those walks,
  and Q2's answer there can at best be "survives only".
- **Its Stage 1 gates G1–G4 pass.**

The owner or the orchestrator chooses one of the following before the four fits start, and records it in
`STAGE1B_PLAN.md` section 6:

- (a) fit it, with Q2 on its brisk and Froude 0.05 walks labelled in advance as limited by the body; or
- (b) drop it from arm K and give its core to the other fits.

After the merge, run `bash stage1/runs/2026-11-10-speed/run_arm_k.sh g1` with nothing else heavy running. It runs G1
again on both K bodies with one process per core (about 1 min each), and replaces their G1 records. G1 then also

This re-run may fail on the step rate alone. The `g1` command's test on the scratch copy (10 Nov, 07:13 UTC, 3
processes, nothing else running) measured:

- `60-N-T-10-K`: 21,081 steps/s alone and 20,125 per process, 0.6 % above the gate;
- `60-N-H-10-K`: 20,986 alone and 21,391 per process.

With one process per core, the rate may well fall below 20,000 on this machine, and G1 says to stop when any of its
checks fails (`STAGE1_PLAN.md` section 3). If that happens, time a pick the same way, into a scratch `--out`, to tell
the machine from the body. Then record what is decided in `STAGE1B_PLAN.md` section 6.

The K records were made before the merge, although decision 7 merges first. They came from a dirty tree at 07a73f4
whose Stage 1 sources were 4b704e4's (`6c58844f`). No re-run beyond G1's is needed, because the products would come
out the same:

- The merge brings `__main__.py`, `env.py`, and the new `speedfit.py` and `speedreport.py`. `__main__.py` gains the
  speed commands and finds `round_two_models` by candidate id, which only `eval` and `sweep` use; its `prepare` and
  `gates` paths are unchanged. `env.py` changes an unlisted body's push bound.
- No module that `prepare` or G1–G4 run imports `env.py`, `speedfit.py` or `speedreport.py`.
- Among the Stage 1 modules, this branch has since changed only comments in `__init__.py`.

## 6. Notes on the records and the code

- **G4's turn-gap note is wrong for the K bodies.** It says "the turning screen's turn gap is the pelvis plate's
  clearance to the same-side thigh ... not a gap between the legs". That holds for Stage 1's bodies but not for
  either K body (the record's `turn_gap.cases` are right):
  - `60-N-T-10-K`'s 4.0 mm is the two hip brackets (`leg_to_leg` true; they sit 4 mm apart at every pose, `legs.py`);
  - `60-N-H-10-K`'s pairs are the power-distribution board against the right lower thigh (0.84 mm), and the pubic
    boot against the right upper thigh (0.28 mm).

  Decision 20's conclusion still holds: the turn gap bounds no step. `gates.py` is left as it is, because it is one of
  the speed fit's frozen sources (decision 5).
- **The knee headroom is missing from the records.** The K candidate records show `headroom` 1.0 and do not record
  `headroom_by_group`, so only the case name (`refine.CASES["narrow_knee_60"]`) says that the knee was sized at 0.85.
  `knee_60`'s records are the same. `refine._size` stores `headroom_by_group` from this branch on. The committed
  records are not sized again for it.
- **The registry does not cover everything.** `plant`, `refs`, `control` and `panels.key_context` look up a body's
  round-two files through `candidate_id`, but:
  - `ledger` (`sweep`) does not. `ledger.screens` reads `<body>_turning.json`, and its per-body tables (`MASSES`,
    `STAGE0`) have no K entries. So the K bodies are not margin-ledger bodies, and none is planned.
  - On this branch alone, `eval` refuses a K body, because `__main__.round_two_models` looks for `<body>.xml`.
    `panels.key_context` also raises KeyError, because `env.PUSH_BOUND_M_S` has no K entry. Main's `__main__.py` and
    `env.py` fix both, so the K bodies' rows run only on the merged code (decision 5).
- **Three tests already failed at 07a73f4.** On this branch the first two fail the same way, and the third is fixed:
  - `test_stage1_ledger.py::test_rows_and_the_bus_voltage_rule` reads the run's R3 state. Main's c94202b fixes it, in
    a hunk that merges cleanly.
  - `test_stage1_policy_rows.py::test_the_ledger_sweeps_the_policy_rows_r3` fails with `STAGE1_POLICY_RUNS` pointing
    at main's run, and is skipped without checkpoints.
  - `test_turning.py::test_zero_yaw_rate_reproduces_gait_generate` failed whenever the whole suite ran in one
    process. It passes alone, but a Stage 1 test run before it leaves round two's walk accuracy set
    (`gait.set_accuracy` is process-wide; `designlab.stage1.refs` sets it). `test_turning.py` now sets round one's
    accuracy for its module and restores the previous one after.

  On this branch the suite gave 261 passed, 33 skipped and the ledger test's failure. Run it again on the merged
  checkout before the fits, `test_stage1_speedfit` included (decision 6).

# Cost basis and physical qualification

The full R3 prototype budget in PR #663 is $16,447 nominal and $21,817 high, including outsourced fabrication, integration and spares. These are baseline estimates. A revised chest bracket, electrical layout, harness and tail spar require fresh quotes. Do not add a new cover allowance to a total that already buys covers.

The comparison in `cover_work.csv` counts each removable mesh panel once. It sums each mid-surface area, solid PA12 wall mass and separate insert/fastener allocation. The illustrative bench fit-up allowance is 4–8 minutes per piece at $60–90 per hour, equivalent to $4–12 per piece. These are explicit scenario assumptions for one assembly pass. Numbers omit print setup, powder, nesting, finishing, shipping, engineering, replacement stock and vendor margin. A shorter piece count can still cost more at the printer if the deep shell claims more build volume. Model exported shapes, choose the powder-bed process and finish, and obtain the same three-vendor quote for each candidate before comparing installed prototype totals.

Use PA12 coupons from the intended process, measure finished density and flex, and weigh panels, paint, real fasteners and retainers. Validate pullout and edge stiffness at bosses, retention of the separate jaw and ribs, blunt exposed tips, reachable pinch gaps, and removal sequence. A geometric wall thickness above a printer's nominal minimum does not establish stiffness across the unsupported ribcage span. [Materialise PA12 SLS design guidelines](https://www.materialise.com/en/industrial/3d-printing-materials/pa12-sls)

1. Build an adjustable spine-and-tray mock-up. Use the actual cabled compute kit, battery with leads and latch, regulators, RGB-D, range boards, fuse/disconnect, and the intended hand tools. Remove each unit on the supported frame. Mark required hatch edges, cable turn radii, contact points and insertion travel.
2. Fit one chest and one hip cover to a loaded joint rig. Check the worst coupled hip roll/pitch and knee bend with real cable loops, bounding fabrication tolerances, fastener heads, foam compression and bracket deflection. Measure maximum unsupported span; select a flange or return at any rattling edge.
3. Weigh each built item and obtain its installed center by balancing or fixture measurements. Reconcile the residual reserve on a scale. Measure actuator current, sag, temperature, torque and output speed under repeated stance, transfer and head positioning at realistic supply voltage and ambient conditions. Test the head pitch axis and tail shaft bending separately.
4. Measure inlet/outlet area after filters, guards and wiring. Log Jetson clocks and temperatures at the intended workload with covers fitted. Check actual stereo fields, ToF masks, head motion and body pitch using physical targets at several heights and ranges.
5. Use harness-supported foot transfers to measure placement, sole contact, lateral reaction, available moment and loaded clearance. Rebuild the contact proxies before committing a changed body to locomotion training. Expand gait and autonomy work after the geometric and drive gates have been met.

# Independent review of the add-on design, AB (16 Nov 2026)

Working paper: a reviewer's findings as returned. Nothing here is a rule.

**Verdict.** Item A (delays): can be registered once the three major points on it are fixed (the cap that no committed file shows, the reading word "up" that ignores whether the robot walks, the choices still open in the rule text). Every statement the design makes about today's runner for item A checked out in the code or by my own calls; no code in a key changes; the cost holds under the 1.0 core-hour cap. Item B (carpet range): NOT registrable as written. The build idea (one new file, no key module edited) is sound and I confirmed it changes no existing key. But the linear floors do not deliver what their names and the range sentence say (one blocker), and six major guards are missing (check-before-commit order with no pass rule, a key that holds the recipe and not the values, an identity check that never exercises a changed floor, an open path to records marked gated, an omitted expectation from Stage 1's low pile, unread secondary figures entering a rule). All fixes are text, tests or the new file; none needs a key module. Hard limits: kept. I wrote nothing in either repository (git status --short: 0 lines in both at the end), ran no episode, no settle, no fit. I ran bar dry-run with --cost and --no-measure (simulates nothing), pure functions, and compile-only model arithmetic (settle=False, mj_kinematics only). One deviation to report: six short scripts that only read committed JSON files with the standard library ran with the venv Python directly, not through tools/lowprio.sh; each took about a second and used no numpy.

## 1. [blocker] design_AB.md 3.4 (floor table), rules B-1 and B-8 (the range sentence), risk table 3.7

**Problem.** BLOCKER for item B. The linear floors are not what their names say, so the range sentence 'the largest sink at which the walk is still up' cannot be read. (1) The named sink holds only with all 16 pad contacts loaded. The law is a linear spring per contact with no bottom, so a foot loaded on fewer contacts sinks 2 to 4 times deeper than the pile the name refers to. (2) 'Softer only' is not softer only: the constant impedance 0.5 also makes the friction rows softer, so the pads creep sideways several times faster at the same friction value. The two knobs are confounded. (3) The study's own module says this law is not for walking. (4) From 1.0 mm of sink the toes, which do not collide, are below the floor plane. (5) The law is applied to 16 box corners, not to the study's probe field, so it is not 'the study's own law for its piles' in geometry.

**Evidence.** Planned figures, my compile-only arithmetic with carpet.static_depth on plant.build('75-M-H-10','hard',settle=False), no physics step: single-foot sink at 16 / 8 / 4 loaded contacts: c0.6 0.60 / 1.20 / 2.40 mm; c1.25 1.25 / 2.50 / 5.00 mm; c2.4 2.40 / 4.80 / 9.60 mm; c4.4 4.40 / 8.80 / 17.6 mm; rfc 2.06 / 2.53 / 3.05 mm (it stiffens). So c2.4 and rfc are not 'about the same sink' once the load moves to an edge, which is the state before every recorded fall (Stage 1 records: fall candidate cop_at_pad_edge in 20 of 20). Creep, a design estimate from MuJoCo's soft-constraint model (slip speed = R x force / b, R = (1-d)/d x 1.018 per kg, b = 2/(d_max x time constant); cone is elliptic, impratio 1, my read of the compiled model), not verified by any step: against the hard floor pair the slip speed per newton is about 3.6 times (c0.6), 5.2 times (c1.25), 7.2 times (c2.4), 3.7 times (rfc at 2 mm). The slip limits are 5 mm median and 15 mm worst (bar.py RULES walk). designlab/carpet.py module docstring, item 1: 'these floors are not suited to touchdown or other dynamic studies, or to controller training, until carpet damping is sourced'. Toe capsules sit 1.00 mm and claws 1.80 to 1.87 mm above the pad soles at the home pose (my mj_kinematics read). carpet.soft_floor returns condim 6 and the study applies it to probes on a 4 mm grid (carpet.py pile_floor, pile_model); heel pad half-size 15.7 x 18.8 mm, toe pads 15.7 x 12.5 mm, each given 4 equal springs.

**Fix.** Before registering: (a) add to the table, per floor, the planned sink at 16, 8 and 4 contacts and the creep factor, each labelled planned or design estimate; (b) name the floors by what is set (stiffness in kPa/mm with all pads flat), not by a pile height; (c) pre-register one more reading per record, from the traces' low points with no code change in a key: the deepest pad penetration before the fall onset (median and largest), printed beside the name, and the slip terms' counts; (d) rewrite the range sentence to list floors by name with their words and measured penetration, and drop 'largest sink'; (e) put carpet.py's sentence, the 16-corner geometry and the toes-below-plane limit into label B-11; (f) say in the rules that 'softer' also changes sideways creep and response time, so a fall on a 'softer only' floor is not attributed to sink.

## 2. [major] design_AB.md rule B-1, B-5, B-8, test 6, point 7 for the lead

**Problem.** The floors' check is placed before the rules are committed and has no pass rule. 'floors check' settles the robot on each floor (physics). Point 7 says it should run before the rules are committed. That puts a result before the rules, and the floor list could then be changed on it. Nothing says how far the simulated sink may differ from the closed-form sink the name rests on. 'The settle's sink' is not defined.

**Evidence.** Stage 1's plant.json (simulated, zero command): root_z_change is -0.236 mm on the hard floor against a closed-form 0.114 mm, and -2.095 mm on robot_floor_carpet against 1.593 mm. The root drop mixes servo sag with pad sink, and the gap is 0.12 mm on one floor and 0.50 mm on the other. Two people would report different 'settle sinks'.

**Fix.** Commit the rules first. Make the check the first registered step (no episode): define the simulated sink as floor plane minus the pads' lowest point at the settled pose; state a band against the closed-form two-feet value (for example 10 % or 0.1 mm, whichever is larger); a floor outside the band or not settled reads 'not run: check failed' and is not retuned or replaced. Print root drop separately.

## 3. [major] design_AB.md 3.3 ('The record key holds the floor by value in key.row.floor'); proto/floors_proto.py lines 105-107

**Problem.** The claim is wrong for the prototype it rests on. The key holds the recipe (name, ('linear', sink in mm), mu, why), not the resolved contact values. The resolved values depend on a data file that is in no key.

**Evidence.** floors_proto.py describe() returns floor=dict(name, **FLOORS[name]). The rfc values (solref 0.024 s / 1.2, solimp 0.65-0.94-4 mm, friction 0.95) and the impedance 0.5 and damping ratio 1.0 of the linear law are read from designlab/data/research/carpet_2026-11-07.json (carpet.floor_variants, carpet.screening_values). panels.key_context (panels.py:1609-1618) hashes the record, MJCF, ledger and clearance files only. So an edit of that JSON would leave a floor record 'up to date' under other floor values.

**Fix.** Put into describe(): the floor geom's resolved priority, condim, solref, solimp, friction; the friction set on every colliding geom; contacts per foot; A_hat; d0; zeta; and the sha256 of the research JSON. Add a test that changing any of them changes the key. Write the same values into floors_check.json and compare.

## 4. [major] design_AB.md 3.4 ('Grippier: the sliding friction, set as _mu0.54 sets it'), rule B-3 (unit B0), tests 2 and 3; floors_proto.py lines 68-72

**Problem.** The identity check proves only the null floor. B0 never exercises set_friction with a new value, apply_floor, or their undo inside an episode. And the grip is not set as the stock runner sets it: the stock path builds and settles the plant, then sets friction; the prototype sets friction, then settles. That is a different start for the 'grippier only' floor than the registered cells it is printed beside.

**Evidence.** panels.Context.plant (panels.py:979-988): plant.build (which settles), then control.set_friction. floors_proto.py: apply_floor, set_friction, then p.settle. Whether the settled pose differs in bits by friction is unknown without a settle; I did not run one.

**Fix.** In floor_on: apply the contact law, settle, then set the friction, as the stock path does. Add a second identity unit, about 0.018 core-hours: R2~h_mu0.54/42@4ms on walk/v0.4 must reproduce, seed by seed and by trace sha256, the registered record R2/42@4ms walk/v0.4_mu0.54 (simulated: 20 up, 0 meet every limit, 11 but for A1). Add a test that floor_on with rfc at 0.54 reproduces a committed Stage 1 robot_floor_carpet episode's trace sha256 (smoke on Stage 1's own run directory). Add a test that an exception inside floor_on leaves the five arrays and the settled pose restored.

## 5. [major] design_AB.md 3.3 ('Why its own command'), test 4; floors_proto.py lines 133-135

**Problem.** The dangerous path stays open. The prototype registers its rows with panels.register_rows at import. Anyone can then run the stock 'bar run --row-module ...floors --rows R2~c1.25_mu0.9/42@4ms'. The stock runner builds Delayed(OnFloor(...)), and the design's own prototype showed the result: records marked gated: true, run on _mu0.54, turns and stops too. Test 4 (settle under a delay wrapper) suggests that nesting is meant to stay possible.

**Evidence.** bar.rows always wraps a registered row in Delayed (bar.py:921-930); Delayed.runs returns True and Delayed.gated returns cond.gated (bar.py:893-897).

**Fix.** Do not register the rows with panels.register_rows. Make OnFloor refuse to run when a delay is already installed on the plant ('command' in p.__dict__) and refuse any wrapper around it. Test both refusals. Drop test 4 or turn it into the refusal test.

## 6. [major] design_AB.md rule B-10 item 4 ('The linear floors ...: no expectation; no record exists')

**Problem.** An expectation that can be stated from known results is not stated. c1.25 is defined as 'the stiffness of the study's low pile' (32.9 kPa/mm), and Stage 1 has 40 episodes of this body walking on that low pile at friction 0.54.

**Evidence.** Simulated, committed: stage1/runs/2026-11-08/75-M-H-10/eval/R2/42/carpet/low.json and R2/43/carpet/low.json: 0 of 20 up under both fits, falls at 4.64 to 11.78 s (median 7.03 s) and 5.33 to 12.43 s (median 8.55 s), so mostly after the start (the start lasts 5.67 s of reference time). Differences to name: probes over the whole underside against 16 pad corners, Stage 1's carpet swing reference, the slow walk, pile depth 2.9 mm.

**Fix.** State it in B-10: for c1.25_mu0.54 (same stiffness per area, same friction) the nearest known record fell 20 of 20 under both fits, so 'down' is expected, with the differences listed; a record that reads up is reported as a contrast with Stage 1. Say what follows for c1.25_mu0.9 and c1.25_mu1.25 (no record at those frictions).

## 7. [major] design_AB.md 3.4 ('2.4 and 4.4 mm are geometric bounds'; 'A bound, not a guess'), friction paragraph

## 8. [major] design_AB.md rule A-3, 2.3 (cap guard in the chain script), point 3 for the lead

**Problem.** The 1.0 core-hour cap of item A cannot be checked from any committed file. The ledger will say task 3 with a cap of 16 core-hours. Every record's sessions will say task 3. A cut made by the chain script leaves no committed trace.

**Evidence.** bar.Budget.load writes cap_core_h = 16.0 for task 3 (bar.py:1800-1810; my call). evaluate writes task=task into every session (bar.py, sess dict). stage2/runs/.gitignore ignores done/ and logs/ (my git check-ignore), and STAGE2_75_PLAN.md H6 says the chain's markers 'are no evidence for a reviewer'. I confirmed Budget(task=5, cap_core_h=1.5) is accepted and that such a call builds the same rows, so the keys stay equal to the stock runner's. If the lead keeps --task 3, the chain must write each cut into a committed file and rule A-3 must name it. State that A's and B's budgets come on top of H6's 16 + 12 core-hours.

## 9. [major] design_AB.md 2.1 (reading words, the patterns), rule A-6

**Problem.** 'Up' counts staying up only. The pattern '10 ms is a special point' can then be read from a robot that stays up without walking as commanded. The one known case above 10 ms is exactly that.

**Evidence.** Simulated, Stage 1 ledger (sweeps/R2/43/latency.json, evaluation latency_steps=10): 3 of 3 up at 20 ms, each failing speed, cadence, stride, ground force and drive speed; speed 0.240 m/s, peak ground force 2.04 body weights, ankle drive at 1.23 to 1.24 of rated speed. The bar's episode records hold success.along and segment_reached (my read of a registered record), so a second word needs no code.

**Fix.** Pre-register a second word per record: 'up and covering the distance' when at least 17 of 20 episodes stay up and meet the along term (for the slow walk: Stage 1's speed term), else 'up, short of the distance'. The 'special point' sentence must carry that word and may say 'a window of staying up, not of walking' when the second word fails. Also say that 17 and 3 were chosen after seeing the registered counts, and that 17 here is not H1's rule (H1 needs 20 of 20 up and 17 that succeed).

## 11. [minor] design_AB.md 0, 2.4, 3.6 (cost)

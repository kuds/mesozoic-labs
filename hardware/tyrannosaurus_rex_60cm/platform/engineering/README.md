# Intermediate T. rex installed hardware

The nominal plant carries **9.896 kg**, including 600 g of unresolved integration mass. The current physical prototype estimate is **$15,621**. Its 6S 3.3 Ah pack reaches the 15–20 minute objective under the design duty estimate. Higher leg effort can reduce runtime below 15 minutes. All three figures require an assembled, instrumented prototype.

## Audit and reproduction

Run these commands from the checkout root with the repository MuJoCo environment. Both tools use the generated model and ledger for each candidate. Every component includes an estimated body-local CG, reference-pose world CG, central inertia and a link to its procurement line. Machined pieces priced together are identified by their shared fabrication lot. Catalog motor mass excludes mounting hardware. Visible internals such as the Jetson carrier or regulator PCB **consume** their assembly allowance. An integration mass reserve carries no separately priced part.

## Head and tail transmissions

Both the head pitch and tail yaw motor sit on stationary forks. A supported shaft carries the shell load into two bearings across the joint. The shafts, bearings and output hubs need detailed drawings, axial retention and bearing fits. Arms and jaw remain fixed. The head's static gravity moment dominates the neck comparison. Tail yaw works against inertia, friction and disturbance on an upright torso. The 82 g XM430-W210-T manufacturer figures at 12 V are 3 N·m stall and 77 rpm unloaded. ROBOTIS quotes **0.6 N·m estimated continuous** using 20% of stall. The plant uses 0.50 sustained and 0.88 N·m peak as planning values. [Servo manual](https://emanual.robotis.com/docs/en/dxl/x/xm430-w210/) · [Supplier estimate and price](https://robotis.us/products/dynamixel-xm430-w210-t)

An eight-second fixed-pelvis bench compares both drives against the mass/inertia and gravity moments from the nominal MJCF. The remote motor rotates a 10 mm input drum. Opposed lines pull 30 mm horn arms across 190 mm final spans. As the arm swings, both the stretched length and moment arm change. The model tracks drum inertia and output angle separately, uses unilateral damped springs at 12 kN/m, assigns 18 N pretension per line, includes guide friction, a position deadband, motor torque-speed limits and output bearing drag. The controller measures the output angle. Motor effort acts only through the drum in the remote case. Dynamics use 0.5 ms steps. A 1 mm slack case with 5 N initial preload loses tension in at least one strand throughout the trial.

The 3:1 nominal transmission lowers neck motor effort. Its input must travel farther. The two cable alternatives together add **152 g**. Each axis budgets 12 g input drum, 24 g independent support/bearings, 12 g guides/line, 18 g tensioners/anchors and 10 g net extra output sector. With the assumed mounts drawn inward, total yaw inertia falls from 0.2277 to 0.2213 kg·m² while CoM rises 3.43 mm. The motor would need a removable side service panel, accessible threaded adjustments, a supported drum, anti-derail flanges and replaceable pre-stretched lines. The complete 0.19 m runs have no installed lane reserved in this plant. Friction and spring parameters lack coupon measurements. The bench holds the pelvis stationary. The mechanically simpler direct drives remain in the walking plant. The historical b4aed804 cable-neck bench tested wrap arcs, an independent remote input and 20 N pretension on a different fixed-tail skeleton and corroborates the mechanism as an option.

## Electrical duty and heat

The 22.2 V, 3.3 Ah pack stores **73.26 Wh nominal**. The design calculation uses 54.95 Wh (75%) and compares 65% and 85%. A loaded-bus stop at 21.0 V is provisional; monitor individual cells and temperature as well as bus voltage. A freshly charged six-cell pack reaches 25.2 V. The RMD drawing permits 20–55 V. Wiring sag, low-cell behavior before the 20 V drive boundary, and regenerative peaks at full charge remain open qualification items. The AK gearsets are rated at 24 V; that voltage alone leaves full-charge and low-voltage operation unverified.

| Assumed duty | Average bus | At 21 V | Runtime at 75% usable | Range at 65–85% usable |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Quiet movement | 106 W | 5.1 A | 31.0 min | 26.9–35.2 min |
| Design walk | 171 W | 8.1 A | 19.3 min | 16.7–21.9 min |
| Strenuous | 246 W | 11.7 A | 13.4 min | 11.6–15.2 min |

Per-side design RMS torques in yaw, roll, hip pitch, knee, ankle pitch and ankle roll are 1.5, 4.2, 4.1, 4.8, 3.0 and 2.6 N·m. Matching mean *positive* output shaft powers are 0.75, 2.3, 3.0, 3.8, 2.4 and 1.3 W. To price a hold, copper loss uses three phases at 0.4 Ω each and current τ/1.8 RMS. Gear efficiency is an assumed 75%; drive conversion 92%; idle 1.4 W/axis. Auxiliary rail delivery of 46 W includes a 25 W Jetson module, 6 W carrier/storage/cooling, 5 W sensors, 4 W MCU/communications and 6 W appendages, through 88% conversion. No credit is taken for regenerative energy. These are assumed operating cases. They do not measure the learned policy or a particular outfit on carpet.

At 75% usable, 20 minutes allows **165 W** average bus input; 15 minutes allows **220 W**. The design case implies 56 W of traction winding loss across twelve motors, with 8.5 W in each knee. Strenuous duty raises the knee estimate to 15.2 W. It also asks 6.4 N·m RMS of the knee, above the simulated sustained planning cap. Measure winding/case/ambient temperature and voltage, torque, speed, motor current and supply current through representative duty and cool-down. Estimate thermal resistance/time constant and winding resistance at temperature separately for each location. For a 31 W compute tray and 10 K stream temperature rise, an ideal mixed-flow lower bound is ~2.6 L/s (5.5 CFM) before duct/leak losses. Record thermal throttling and fan RPM with the cover fitted. No steady temperature or physical current limit is qualified here.

Use separate protected branches for traction and auxiliary logic. The proposed compute buck, Pololu D36V50F12, provides 12 V in the Jetson carrier's range. Its typical 4.5 A output is temperature and input dependent. Use a separate 12 V D42V110F12 for the two XM430s (combined stall 4.6 A at 12 V), plus a 5 V D24V10F5 for the MCU and compatible peripherals. The actual rail loads, continuous buck ratings at 21–25.2 V, USB current and sequencing need a loaded prototype. Retain a hardware traction disconnect independent of Jetson, protect each branch, instrument bus and cells, prevent external-source backfeed, and design a controlled path to dissipate regenerated energy with a full or disconnected pack. Validate fuse coordination against wire/connector limits and fault current. Integrated actuator phase-current ratings cannot be added to obtain an average pack draw. A separate four-second free-standing crouch replays the same joint targets on the same fully loaded plant at 21.0 V, 22.2 V and 24.0 V. The last configuration retains the isolated pack. The simulation produces 1.32 W mean positive leg shaft output and 35.2 mm peak root drop. Combining full-resolution motor torque and speed with the electrical assumptions yields 88.5 W average / 95.2 W peak total in all three cases. This slow, low-speed trajectory does not exhaust the voltage-dependent speed envelope. Walking or fast reversal would be a different demand. The electrical budget above is the basis for runtime.

For a supported test stand, a 24 V, 31.3 A Mean Well RSP-750-24 is a candidate off-board supply. Our illustrative 550 W transient needs 22.9 A at 24 V or 26.2 A at 21 V. A constant-voltage supply may reject reverse current, so the dump clamp/load and undervoltage behavior must work on external power. Retain the **650 g installed battery assembly**, electrically isolated, or fit a characterized cassette with the same installed mass, CG and inertia. The training plant always carries that mass. ## Fabrication and budget

Route load from each sole through replaceable elastomer pads and a stiff forefoot plate into a double-shear ankle yoke, shank rails, knee/hip bearings, pelvis webs and spine crossmembers. Each flanged motor transmits joint torque. Crossmembers and separately supported bearings should carry the side and overturning loads. For the nominal 9.90 kg allocation, a screening case of 2× body weight places 194 N on one foot. A 35 mm force offset produces ~6.8 N·m; an effective 35 mm bearing spread introduces ~194 N of opposing bearing force before the direct radial reaction. These are load cases for the bracket designer. MuJoCo capsules and planning cylinders do not specify a load-rated assembly.

Start with laser-/waterjet-cut and bent aluminum webs for simple structure, CNC-machined bearing seats and spacers on critical datums, and external SLS/MJF PA12 skull, fixed arms and panels. Specify hole datum relationships, reaming/bearing fit and retention, fastener grip, thread engagement, access from a tool side and metrology through the joint travel. A carbon tube or sheet option can lighten the shanks, but inserts, bonded overlap, impact inspection and joint slip need coupons; apparent bare tube mass overstates the saving. Keep compliance and replaceable pads in the sole. Test lateral shear and toe catching on short pile. Dimensional stacks, fatigue, gear/output bearing limits, vibration isolation, shell edge treatment, cable flex life, fastener preload and drop loads still need fabrication CAD, supplier review and physical tests.

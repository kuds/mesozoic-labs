# Tyrannosaurus Rex (Robot)

The mini M1 skeleton promotes torso A and the forward-folding ten-servo legs
into a registered SB3 environment. Its 754.1 g mass is an engineering allocation,
including 115 g of fixed future head/neck, tail and arm inertia allowances.
The tail has no joint or actuator. No physical prototype or learned walking is
claimed. M2 integration and M3 production readiness are later hardware milestones.

## Training

Choose `SPECIES = "tyrannosaurus_rex"`, `EMBODIMENT = "robot"`,
`BEHAVIOR = "stance"` and `QUICK_TEST = True` in the SB3 notebook first.

```bash
python -m environments.shared.train --species tyrannosaurus_rex --embodiment robot train --stage stance
```

The four recipes are stance, push recovery from stance, locomotion from stance,
and target reaching from locomotion. The legacy `hunt` recipe label means slow,
upright arrival within 4 cm of a planar target; it does not mean biting.
The 41 observations include privileged simulator velocity, target and foot-force
signals. Ten home-relative position commands pass through a 10 Hz action filter
at a 50 Hz control rate. Actual motor and sensor identification is pending.

The recovery calibration measures fixed-command PD baselines in simulation.
Budgets and qualification thresholds remain experimental, and a short training
run establishes software integration only. Every trained checkpoint retains its
own plant/task identity and normalization statistics.

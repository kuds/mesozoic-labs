# Mini Tyrannosaurus Rex: silhouette-C character prototype

The [standalone example](../../examples/mini_tyrannosaurus_rex/README.md) targets a
roughly 27 cm robot with forward knees, a light T-Rex head, visible mechanisms and
moving arms. The revised architecture has **14 active XL330-M288-T servos**:
ten leg motors, head yaw and nod, jaw, and one shared arm drive. The two-axis head
replaces the previous four-axis neck/head stack. Both arms share one command;
independent left/right gestures are outside this revision.

An **optional 825 g / 15-motor tail-yaw configuration** adds a small side-to-side
wag without changing the 800 g base XML. It is generated by
[tail_option.py](../../examples/mini_tyrannosaurus_rex/tail_option.py). Its mass,
power and validation results are separate from the base configuration below.

The **800 g design target is an unweighed allocation**. This experimental MJCF
is outside the registered training inventory. It is not fabrication CAD, a
demonstrated walker or a hardware-validated power design. A rendering or short
numerical hold does not establish those capabilities.

The current silhouette-C home exterior is approximately **267.9 mm high x
160.6 mm wide x 267.0 mm long**. It uses a rounded tapered skull, larger side
eyes and a cream jaw, with full-size motor cases and revision-10 joint pivots.
The moving head-yaw subtree retains its 91 g allocation; updated surface/hardware
moments are recorded in [the head allocation](../../examples/mini_tyrannosaurus_rex/head_allocation.json).
These targets do not establish manufactured shell mass or cost.

The retained mechanism moves each hip-roll shaft 15 mm outward while retaining
full motor cases, and reduces the forward-knee offset from 14.5 to 10 mm. Home
static loads under the documented support assumption fall from approximately
0.0879 to **0.0369 Nm at hip roll** and 0.0618 to **0.0491 Nm at the knee**.
The narrower bend reduces extension/crouch travel, and settling transfers some
load to the ankles. In a ten-second ideal hold with 0.10 Nm hard leg caps,
final-second means with the revised stance gains reach approximately 0.0529 Nm
at the knee and 0.0204 Nm at the ankle. These are simulation results, not measured
motor duty. Leg position gains are half and velocity gains one quarter of
revision 10; unchanged torque limits now support a 30-second hold with less than
1.8 mm root drift for both configurations and no settled clipping.

The [reproducible report](../../examples/mini_tyrannosaurus_rex/validation.json)
includes 2,000 sampled full-body poses, 625 combined head/arm poses, motor-box
clearance and lateral-transfer diagnostics. No sampled non-floor collision-proxy
penetrations were found at the retained limits. The simple static lateral-transfer
strategy still fails its sole-bound test; walking remains an open controller and
mechanism question rather than a demonstrated capability.

## Mechanical architecture and access

Keep the blunt snout, tiny two-finger hands, rounded chest and light tail as the
character's identifying features. An internal pelvis/spine should carry the leg
and neck loads. Keep hip and shoulder mechanisms visible. The cream belly and
rear panel are intended as removable service covers for the battery connector,
computer, power distribution and wiring. Shown seams and fasteners remain
concepts until CAD defines openings, wall thickness, bosses, tool access and the
removal sequence. The decorative shell must not be the only structural load path.

Mount the shared arm motor in the torso and connect it to two supported shoulder
pivots through a shaft or bellcranks. Avoid putting a full motor inside each tiny
arm. The simulated coupling is not a manufactured linkage or a model of its
losses and backlash. Verify the complete sweep against chest, jaw, cables and
covers before increasing travel.

The arm motor and jaw motor are offset from their driven hinges; neither is a
coaxial direct drive. Their offset transmission geometry still requires CAD.
The two head axes have separate full-size case locations and shaft alignments.
Current command limits are yaw +/-0.65 rad, nod -0.25 to +0.10, jaw 0 to +0.32,
and linked shoulder pitch -0.65 to +0.20. The unactuated shoulder's simulator
encoder channels are not independent hardware measurements.

For two 10 g arms with a conservative 25 mm center-of-mass lever, horizontal
gravity torque is approximately **0.00245 Nm per arm**, **0.0049 Nm combined** at
a 1:1 transmission, before friction and acceleration. A slow gesture is a small
motor load; packaging and whole-body mass are the meaningful constraints. Set a
low arm effort limit after measuring linkage losses, rather than inheriting the
leg's transient allowance. The base tail is fixed; the optional wag is a
character gesture, not a validated active balancing controller. Keep
battery/computer mass in the torso and heavy electronics out of the snout.

## Optional tail-yaw module

The option adds one **18 g XL330-M288-T** plus a **7 g mounting, bearing and
wiring allowance**, increasing assigned mass from 800 to **825 g** and actuator
count from 14 to **15**. The existing **27.9 g integration reserve is retained**;
the added hardware is not hidden inside it. The shell stays at an unweighed 15 g
allocation while its geometry scales to 90% of the base tail. Its assigned COM
is 45 mm behind its pivot and its yaw inertia about that pivot is
5.3055e-5 kg m^2. A camera is excluded from both mass configurations.

The current layout places the tail pivot at **(-83, 0, 15) mm** relative to the
torso frame and the full-size motor case center at **(-75.5, 0, -29.5) mm**. A
schematic 30 mm extension and root bearing connect the drive to the tail. The
already-budgeted 70 g battery moves 23 mm forward; the torso COM/inertia are
updated for both the battery relocation and added hardware. This is a rear
module design study, not a completed mount or a demonstrated battery fit.

The motor belongs on the torso, with a supported tail pivot so that impacts and
cantilever bending do not rely only on the servo output. Upright yaw motion has
no gravity torque about a vertical axis, but the tail still loads the bearing in
bending. For the assigned tail, a conservative 30-degree body lean plus a
25-degree, 1 Hz sinusoidal wag gives approximately **0.0042 Nm** combined
gravity/inertial torque before friction and actuator losses. This is an analytic
sizing estimate, not a motor-duty measurement. A heavier tail, payload at its
tip, contact with furniture or a fall can dominate the gentle-wag load.

Keep **+/-25 degrees** as the modeled travel limit and start character motion at
**+/-15 degrees, 0.5 Hz**. The ideal actuator has a provisional 0.02 Nm command
cap; this is not a calibrated hardware current limit. The current
[tail validation report](../../examples/mini_tyrannosaurus_rex/tail_validation.json)
records:

| Check | Result | Scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full-size motor cases | 2,001 sampled poses; no overlap; minimum separating-axis gap 2.5 mm | Box proxies, not full horns/connectors or tolerance analysis |
| Combined collision proxies | 2,000 sampled poses and 243 upper-body grid poses; no penetration | Retained model collision rules/exclusions |
| Tail against selected service envelopes | 101 tail angles; minimum separation 4.85 mm, at the computer envelope | Tail mesh/motor against those boxes only; not simultaneous whole-robot packing |
| Unsupported standing wag | 10 s at +/-15 degrees and 0.5 Hz; peak tail torque 0.00101 Nm, RMS 0.000735 Nm; maximum torso tilt 1.99 degrees | Ideal position servos, no electrical/thermal validation |
| Home equilibrium | Hip roll about 0.03879 Nm, knee about 0.05438 Nm | One stated static contact-load distribution |

For comparison, the base's corresponding home estimates are about
0.03695/0.04908 Nm at hip roll/knee. The tail option therefore **exceeds the
preliminary 0.05 Nm knee static-load goal**, even though the short stance checks
pass. The added character feature consumes some leg reserve. Motor capacity,
regulator margin and cable/shaft/bearing clearances remain to be qualified.

## Power and wiring

Use separate regulated 5 V motor and logic branches with a common reference
ground. U2D2 carries communications, not motor power. Provide power branches to
each leg and the character actuators; do not feed the whole robot through one
small servo pigtail. Rate all conductors and connectors for their branch loads.
Keep the body IMU rigidly mounted, away from regulator heat/high-current wiring.

The D24V90F5 is advertised around 9 A, but its description also lists typical
continuous capability of 4–8 A and makes output dependent on voltage, airflow
and heat dissipation. Dropout is load dependent. **Its name is not a validated
9 A enclosed-robot rating.** Qualify it with the actual enclosure/harness at the
lowest intended battery voltage. The D24V22F5's 2.5 A figure is also input/thermal
dependent; verify that it meets the Radxa minimum 5 V/2 A supply requirement with
the selected peripherals. These regulators are candidates, not qualified parts.

Battery selection remains open. The starting architecture is a **2S pack with a
matched charger**, regulated to both 5 V branches. A fully charged 2S lithium
pack can reach 8.4 V and must not connect directly to an XL330's 6 V maximum
input. The battery row is a sourcing constraint, not a verified pack. Require
documented discharge capability, rated connectors and appropriate low-voltage
cutoff. Account for converter dropout under load when choosing that cutoff.

Fourteen published 1.47 A stall currents sum to **20.58 A / 102.9 W at 5 V**.
This is a simultaneous-stall arithmetic envelope, not expected walking draw or
a request to operate stalled motors. Firmware current/motion limits, a hardware
motor cutoff and measured concurrent loads must define the operating envelope.
Converter protection is not a configurable whole-robot current budget. MJCF
effort limits are neither firmware settings nor calibrated current-to-torque
conversions.

The optional tail's fifteenth motor changes that arithmetic envelope to
**22.05 A / 110.25 W at 5 V**, adding 1.47 A of possible stall current. A small
wag's low simulated torque does not prove electrical headroom: measure the
combined leg, head, arm and tail load through the actual supply and harness.
The optional configuration has no qualified regulator/current margin yet.

Start on a current-limited bench supply with the robot supported. Log branch
currents, input/rail voltages and servo temperatures during stand, weight transfer
and combined head/arm/jaw gestures. Keep permitted demand below the smallest
tested capability of regulator, harness, connectors and battery, with a documented
transient margin. A current limit that causes loss of balance fails the operating
test. Runtime remains unknown until a pack is selected and average input power
measured; calculate usable battery Wh / average input W and verify a discharge
cycle to the chosen cutoff.

## Compute and sensing

Use headless ARM64 Linux on the Radxa. Its RK3566 has four Cortex-A55 cores up to
1.6 GHz. Train externally and benchmark exported inference, estimator, drivers
and motor bus together. Initial targets are 200–400 Hz body-IMU sampling and a
50 Hz policy loop; hardware rates have not been demonstrated here.

Use 3.3 V host logic. The Adafruit breakout supports SPI/I2C; STEMMA QT carries
I2C while SPI requires appropriate pads including DO and CS. Verify Radxa pinmux
and Linux device configuration. The optional head IMU can initially use I2C.
A Pico bridge would require firmware and does not by itself replace the
direction-controlled half-duplex motor interface.

Encoders and body gyro/accelerometer readings can be hardware observations.
Servo input current is useful telemetry, not a calibrated external torque sensor.
Simulator quaternions, whole-body COM and world-frame foot coordinates remain
privileged diagnostics. A six-axis IMU does not provide drift-free absolute yaw;
one FSR per foot cannot measure center of pressure. Deployment needs a state
estimator and calibrated actuator delay, backlash, speed/load limits and supply
variation. This example has no trained walking policy or hardware deployment
driver; the exploratory controller below is a simulation diagnostic.

### Eyes, cameras and physical observations

The two dark eyes are **cosmetic geometry**. They contain no physical sensors.
The MJCF's forward snout camera is a virtual rendering camera with a 75-degree
vertical field of view; its optical origin sits about 9 mm beyond the current
snout shell. It is not an installed camera, packaged lens opening or calibrated
hardware view. Body/head IMU sites likewise represent proposed measurements,
not wired and tested breakouts. No foot force sensor is implemented.

A practical first camera candidate is **Raspberry Pi Camera Module 2 (IMX219)**,
which Radxa explicitly supports on ZERO 3. Raspberry Pi lists approximately
25 x 24 x 9 mm and 3 g for the bare module, with published horizontal/vertical
fields of view of **62.2/48.8 degrees**, not the model's 75-degree vertical view.
Use the correct cable for Radxa's 22-pin, 0.5 mm CSI connector, verify orientation,
enable the matching overlay and test the actual capture pipeline.

Reserve **8–12 g installed** for camera, mount, cable and small window as an
engineering allowance; that mass is **excluded from the 800 g and 825 g models**.
It must consume documented remaining reserve or increase total mass, and it
must update head/body inertia wherever the hardware is mounted. Keep the eyes
decorative and put the first lens behind an intentional snout/front-head window
if CAD confirms fit. Verify cable bends through all head angles and unobstructed
view across the jaw sweep. Calibrate optical pose, intrinsics and actual capture
mode before matching the simulator camera; module FOV alone is insufficient.
Image processing also needs an explicit CPU, latency and logic-power budget.

## Walking evidence and CI scope

The [walking report](../../examples/mini_tyrannosaurus_rex/walking_validation.json)
records a manual ideal-actuator stepping diagnostic, **not validated walking**.
Its fixed targets and feedback use exact simulator orientation/noiseless gyro,
without a hardware estimator, motor torque-speed curves, supply limits, delay,
backlash or disturbances. The revised servo gains repair stationary creep;
all twelve walking cases were replayed without retuning their target trajectory.

| Configuration and leg cap | Observed result | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Silhouette-C base, 0.20 Nm | Reaches 30 s; 564 mm forward; 150 support alternations | Nominal stepping, with internal contacts and joint-limit overshoot |
| Silhouette-C tail centered, 0.20 Nm | Reaches 30 s; 470 mm forward; 142 alternations | No robust gait acceptance |
| Silhouette-C tail wagging, 0.20 Nm | Falls at 3.592 s | Keep character wagging stationary until gait qualification |
| All three configurations at 0.30 Nm | Reach 30 s | Higher assumed cap does not establish hardware or legal-contact feasibility |
| Base/tail cases at 0.10 or 0.15 Nm | All fall | The controller fails at those lower caps |

Five of twelve trials complete the horizon, but none is a walking certificate.
The completed 0.20 Nm base run still hits a hip link against a motor-case proxy
(about 6.55 N peak) and overshoots a joint limit by about 0.0242 rad. The
[historical report](../../examples/mini_tyrannosaurus_rex/baseline_rev10/walking_validation.json)
preserves revision 10; its tail-wag replay in the current runtime lasts 21.688 s,
compared with its historical 29.616 s. That old plant retains different head
moments and gains. Single trajectories cannot rank robust gait quality.

The 0.20/0.30 Nm caps are not continuous hardware ratings. Measure motor
response, power and thermal performance before choosing operating limits.
The ten-second standing character tests pass for both configurations, with
head-nod peak below 0.0203 Nm and jaw peak below 0.0071 Nm under unchanged
0.10 / 0.025 Nm caps. Those results do not qualify walking with gestures.

CI runs **30-second unsupported stance regressions** with at most 0.10 Nm
leg caps and small prescribed roll/pitch angular-velocity kicks. It checks
finite state, height, tilt, drift, real foot-to-floor contact, external-force
absence and settled effort. Hidden world/fixture constraints, gravity
compensation, disabled gravity/contact physics and unbounded actuators are
rejected. Deliberately weak legs must fail. A half-timestep case checks numerical
sensitivity. The settled effort ceilings are 0.09 Nm RMS and 10% cap contact
(samples at or above 99% of the cap); the revised holds record no such clipping.
These are simulation regressions, not continuous-duty qualification.

Example-only changes trigger CI, local hooks cover the same sources as CI, and
source-hash tests reject stale current reports. The cross-species
[walking-validation proposal](ROBOT_WALKING_VALIDATION.md) describes constrained
limb/load feasibility and the additional gait measurements needed next.

## Verification gates

These are proposed acceptance gates, not reported hardware test results.

1. **CAD/mass:** include exact horns/idlers, bearings, connectors and cable bends.
   Sweep combined legal motions with tolerance/deflection allowances. Demonstrate
   belly/rear panel removal without dismantling a leg. Reconcile measured parts
   to the ledger; exceeding 800 g requires renewed dynamics checks.
2. **Loaded leg/neck duty:** run the intended duty for at least 20 minutes and
   until temperature is approximately stable. Initial project screening gate:
   no protection events, less than 1 degree C rise over the final five minutes,
   and at least 10 degrees C below configured shutdown. This does not qualify
   motor life or continuous torque; repeat at intended warmer ambient conditions.
3. **Effort reserve:** seek home holding demand below 0.05 Nm per leg joint as an
   initial goal, checking external loads/levers as well as simulated torque.
   ROBOTIS's approximately 0.10 Nm continuous estimate is not a certified duty
   envelope. Test one-foot support/transitions separately from two-foot standing.
4. **Power:** at the lowest intended battery voltage and simultaneous motion,
   require rails within device limits at their connectors, no reboot, converter
   shutdown or motor communications fault. Record current/temperature margins.
   Verify motor cutoff with the robot mechanically supported.
5. **Motion/calibration:** measure loaded output-angle error through reversals
   using an external reference; encoder increments are not mechanical accuracy.
   Verify real arm-linkage ratio/losses/travel. Include simultaneous character
   motion in balance testing and later training.
6. **Control:** establish stance and repeatable weight transfers with realistic
   actuators/observations before walking. Evaluate across seeds/disturbances;
   record falls, slip, speed, saturation and power. Similar DOF counts do not
   make a MicroDuck policy compatible with this forward-knee morphology.

If reserve remains inadequate after reducing unnecessary mass and lever arms,
bench-test stronger motors at the loaded joints. The same-envelope XC330-M288-T
is a candidate, not an approved substitution: added mass/current and changed
motor dynamics require a new ledger, thermal checks and actuator identification.

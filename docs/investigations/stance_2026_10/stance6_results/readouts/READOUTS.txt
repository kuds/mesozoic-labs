INFERRED ANSWER (could entropy be driving it?) [inferred, from E1-E7]
- Direct effect: no. The entropy term's gradient reaches log_std only, with the same push on all 15 actuators, and
  never reaches the mean action (from E7). The ledger's rails are the deterministic policy's mean command (and M6's
  'mu beyond the bound'); nothing in the entropy term singles out tail_3 or neck_pitch.
- As a necessary cause: the records argue against it. s60's tail drive crossed the bound, and s42's rail re-formed
  twice, with ent_coef exactly 0 (E2, E5); r9's two railed runs kept 12 and 17 flagged evaluations after 7M (E6).

  most (E4), and std shows no kink at 7M (E4) - but by 6-7M the bonus is below 0.0007, so its removal is gradual. The

  onset cannot be judged from runs that all share one schedule: there is no contrast in these records. n = 3 (and
  r9's 5) runs; GPU training is not bit-reproducible [measured, EVID].
- A plausible mechanism that does not need entropy [inferred, from E7]: once the mean passes the bound, every sample on
  that side clips to the same command, the saturation cost stops growing at |a| = 1, and the clipped surrogate has little
  to pull the mean back; the observed std shrinking fastest on s42's tail_3 fits that. This is a reading, not a test.

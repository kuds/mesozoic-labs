The failure is not a heading bug, and it is not the free SR3 tail amplifying differences either. Float32 rounding in the observation and policy path occasionally turns float64 rotation noise (about 1e-13) into a tiny action difference. The closed loop then carries it at a bounded level, and two of the test's tolerances cannot absorb even one such event on the T. rex. I built r9 and SR3 scratch trees with git archive 9f2355a3. In every file the test depends on, they are identical to om_r9 and om_sr3rc: the only differences are the XML springrefs and _TAIL_SETTLED_QPOS. Q1 reran the test's own paired-heading check on seeds 3042-3061 with torch at 1 thread. r9 fails 7 of 20 seeds (20 of 100 pairs) and SR3 fails 6 of 20 (16 of 100), so r9 passing seeds 3042-3043 is luck (Fisher p=1.0 on seeds). Every failing pair on both trees trips only REWARD_ATOL (1e-9) and CLOSED_LOOP_OBSERVATION_ATOL (1e-5); no pair exceeds the action or joint bounds. In every failing pair at least one action element differs. Before that first action difference, joint state never grows above about 2.7e-13. Q2 traced SR3 seed 3042 at +45 deg. The first action difference is at step 759: the float64 l_hip_pitch:qvel straddles a float32 rounding boundary (heading 0 is 2.3e-14 above it, +45 deg is 4.0e-14 below it), while joint state still differs by only 9.8e-14. Joint state then jumps to 2.2e-9, the reward first exceeds 1e-9 at step 762, and the 3.05e-5 observation difference is one float32 ulp of a 418 N foot force. A state audit and a code read found no world-yaw dependence. Q3: under zero action, a 1e-12 tail or knee perturbation decays at the same rate on both trees (about 0.15 decades per 100 steps). Under the policy it either stays absorbed or causes an action difference that saturates at about 1e-9 qpos, and whether it does tracks the seed, not the tree. With torch at 4 threads, the earlier pytest failure values are reproduced bit for bit, while the set of failing headings changes with the thread count. The real pytest on my trees (1 thread): SR3 traces test fails, SR3 floor-truth metrics test passes, r9 passes both. The fix belongs in the test (a closed-loop reward bound and an observation bound sized to float32 resolution, or a two-phase or open-loop design); nothing in the physics, gate or policy interface needs to change.

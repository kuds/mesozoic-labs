0. VERDICT IN SHORT
==================================================================================================================
- The probe's measurements reproduce bit-for-bit with my own code.
  - Its network evaluator is the SB3 harness; mine reads policy.pth directly and uses my own unpickler.
  - Closed loop: 460 of 460 episodes match by condition, policy and seed.  Return differs by at most 1.1e-11; own
    command, tail angle and loop gain match exactly.
  - Static: 73 of 73 checkpoints; the stored states are byte-identical.
  - The 2x2, the swaps, the grafts and the first-layer column all match.
- The statistics swap was really applied.  The network received the donor's statistics on exactly the intended
  dimensions and on no others.
- Observation indices and the VecNormalize statistics are CONFIRMED independently.
- The headline direction holds.  At fixed weights, the drift of the tail_3 statistics lowers the loop gain.  It does
  not raise it.  This is 12/12 in every order and every sweep range I tried.  The weights carry more than all of the
  gain growth.
- What is overstated, all in the "how much" part:
  (a) The share -0.89 is not a property of the policies.  It is tied to the 0-6 deg sweep.
    - Under the start's statistics, the 0-6 deg sweep drives z_tail3 up to +7.1, where tanh compresses.  The response
      becomes curved.
    - The symmetric share is -1.32 for a 0-3 deg sweep, -0.90 for 0-6, -0.47 for 3-6 and -0.26 for 4.5-6 deg.  The
      4.5-6 deg range sits around the rail's operating point of +5.2 deg.
    - Its sign is negative in all ranges, 12/12.
  (b) The claim "without the drift, 8 (closed loop) / 9 (static) of 12 ends would have loop gain above 1" does not
    hold up.
    - It is range-dependent: 12/12 for 0-3 deg, 9/12 for 0-6, 2/12 for 3-6, 1/12 for 4.5-6.
    - It is also not a "without the drift" counterfactual.  The end weights co-evolved with the drifted statistics.
      With the start's statistics they run at z6 of about +6.1 in closed loop, against about +2.1 under their own
      statistics.  That is outside the range they were trained on.
  (c) The sensitivity growth per normalised unit (Jz) of x5.4 compares slopes over different z ranges.  On matched z
    ranges it is x4.3 to x4.65.  That is still well above the x2.29 that compensating the scale alone would need.
  (d) "A shrinking scale can only lower the gain" holds for the variance part.  The mean part of the drift acts the
    other way.
    - The drifted mean raises the gain in 12/12, at both start and end weights.
    - The effect is +0.02 at the end variance and +0.14 at the start variance, so it depends on the order.  The
      probe's "mean moves the gain by under 0.03" is order-dependent.
  (e) In s80 the 0-6 deg sweep reaches the normaliser's clip (|z| = 10) at 3.1-4.0 deg.  48% of the sweep points are
    clipped.
    - So the s80 gain values are about half of the unclipped slope.
    - The probe's "no tail value comes near the clip" is true for s42 only.
    - s80's "inert tail statistics" still holds over the unclipped 0-2.5 deg range.

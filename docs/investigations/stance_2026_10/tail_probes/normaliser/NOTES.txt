6. READING  [inferred; basis given]
==================================================================================================================
- The hypothesis predicted a falling input scale x fixed weights = rising gain.  The scale did fall, but a smaller
  scale shrinks the network's response to a raw tail angle: gain = Jz x scale.  The network is close to linear here:
  restoring the start's variance raises the ends' gain x2.07, where a linear network would give x2.29.
  So drift of this kind can only lower the gain at fixed weights, and it did (0.27 -> 0.14 for the start's weights).
- The growth is a change in the weights (Jz x5.4, in L2 and the output layer).  It more than offsets the x0.44 input
  compression.  Basis: sections 3-5.
- The causal arrow between the tail and the statistics: the statistics drifted because the tail rose.
  - The added frames sit at +2.8 to +3.9 deg, against the start's 0.47 deg running mean.
  - So the drift is a consequence of the rail behaviour.
  - That is also why the drifting dimension is the one that saturates: the normaliser is a record of where the
    observation went.
  - Basis: implied_window.txt; the start policy already holds the tail near +3 deg on its own.
- The drift probably even made the s42 rail less pronounced than the weights alone would make it.  Without it, 8 of
  12 ends (closed loop) or 9 of 12 (static) would have a loop gain above 1.  Basis: section 5, end W with start stats.

7. WHAT THESE DATA CANNOT SHOW
==================================================================================================================
- Whether the drift shaped the training trajectory.
  - As the input compressed, PPO may have grown the downstream weights partly to compensate.
  - Full compensation needs x2.3; the weights grew x5.4 per normalised unit.  So compensation cannot be the whole
    story.  But part of the weight growth may be a response to the drift.
  - Only a training counterfactual can settle it, for example a continuation with frozen statistics.  None was run
    (no training allowed).
- Why the weights changed (which reward gradient).  The probe localises the change (L2 and the output layer), not its
  cause.
- One start checkpoint per lineage.  No other lineage that drifts the tail_3 statistics without railing.  s80 does
  not drift them, so it cannot separate "drift" from "rail" either.
- The loop gain is local and static.
  - It is an observation-only sweep of the tail_3 angle (0-6 deg) at visited states, times the statue's static plant
    gain.
  - It ignores the velocity channel, the 10 Hz filter, the other observations' response to the tail, and the critic.
  - The per-checkpoint sweep uses seed 3042 only.  The 20-seed closed-loop gains agree within 0.01 (s42).  In s80
    they are 0.01-0.06 more negative (median 0.03).
- Deterministic policies only.  Reset noise 0.05.  No pushes.  No own-noise rollouts.
- A swap moves every swapped input's operating point at once.  The mean/variance split is static only (one dimension,
  seed 3042 states).
- Multiplicity: many keys were read.  The firm results are the 12/12 and 0/12 ones.

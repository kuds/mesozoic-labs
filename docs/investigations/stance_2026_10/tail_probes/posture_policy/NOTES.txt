0. ANSWER IN SHORT
==================================================================================================================
- Yes, in the s42 lineage there is a posture-to-tail pathway, and it is what feeds the tail loop.  It is a learned
  response, not a mechanical push: the plant is the same in every posture.
  - The s42 networks raise the tail command when they SEE the pelvis more nose-down (gravity vector and prey
    direction in pelvis coordinates) and when the knee and hip pitch angles sit where the s42 lineage holds them
    (about 0.5 deg more negative than the s80 lineage's).  s42 ends: -0.157 command per deg of
    nose-up (0/12 positive), i.e. 1 deg of nose-down is worth 1.26 deg of observed tail angle [measured, sweep.txt].
  - It sets the loop's OFFSET, not its gain.  Giving the s42 ends the s80 ends' typical orientation and leg angles
    (all within 1.3 normaliser SD) removes almost their whole offset: c0 0.40 -> about 0.00 (change -0.39
    [-0.76, -0.24], 0/12), gain unchanged (+0.01), linear fixed point -1.2, predicted rail 9/12 -> 0/12; the control
    (own lineage's typical values) moves c0 by -0.03 (4/8) [measured, sweep_extra.txt].
  - The loop (gain 0.63) then multiplies that offset by about 1/(1 - G) = 2.7.  With no offset the loop has nothing
    to amplify [inferred from the linear fixed-point arithmetic; the closed-loop arms below agree in direction].
  - Closed loop: showing the s42 ends a pelvis 1.1 deg more nose-up than it is (only the orientation channels)
    lowers the tail command in 11 of 12 policies (12/12 on the raw mean) and cuts railed episodes 0.67 -> 0.15;
    the reverse (1.1 deg more nose-down) pushes it up (raw +0.085, 11/1) and railed episodes 0.67 -> 0.90.  The
    gravity vector alone does a third of it (railed 0.67 -> 0.39); the prey direction in pelvis coordinates, which
    a pelvis pitch also rotates, carries the rest [measured, analysis_cl.txt].  Over 20 s (5 seeds) the illusion's
    effect holds: railed episodes at 15-20 s 37/60 -> 8/60, command -0.155 (0/12) [measured, analysis_long.txt].
- The s80 lineage has no such pathway (pitch slope -0.019, 5/7 split; transplanting the s42 posture into s80 ends
  moves c0 by -0.02, 5/7) and its loop is negative (G -0.28).  Shown a nose-down pelvis in closed loop, s80 ends
  raise the command by only +0.025 and no rail forms (0 of 240 episodes) [measured].
- Start against ends (s42): the pathway was already there at the start (pitch slope -0.090 vs -0.157 at the ends;
  orientation + legs transplant from s80 lowers the start's c0 by 0.16-0.21 of its 0.41), while the loop gain was
  0.26 vs 0.63.  So between start and ends the posture pathway grew about 1.7x and the loop gain about 2.4x; at the
  start the fixed point was 0.55 (no rail), at the ends 1.04 (railed in 9 of 12 by the linear rule, 0.67 of
  episodes in closed loop) [measured, sweep.txt, sweep_extra.txt].
- The plant does not force the tail up or make it measurably cheaper in the s42 posture (test 3).  In the
  postures the s42 ends actually hold, the tail_3 angle reached is q3 = +0.026 + 5.16 a deg, the same as the statue's (+0.026 + 5.16 a)
  and the s80 ends' (+0.024 + 5.15 a); the command needed to hold the tail_home target is -0.0007 (statue -0.0006);
  gravity on tail_3 differs by < 0.01 N m of 14.49; tail-up (a = +1 against 0) costs 0.0989 reward per step in the
  s42 posture, 0.0986 on the statue and 0.0984 in the s80 posture; the tail-tip gyro is about 0.001 rad/s and
  tail_stability pays under 0.0001 per step for it either way [measured, analysis_plant.txt].  A pure 1 deg
  nose-down rotation of the held posture raises the railed tail_3 angle by 0.013 deg (worth 0.0025 command units)
  and lowers the command needed to hold the target by 0.0007: the sign that "helps" tail-up, but 1/60 or less of
  the 0.157 command the policy's own pitch pathway adds per degree [measured, plant_pitch.txt, sweep.txt].
- So the answer to the maintainer: the habit tracks a body positioning, but through the policy's perception of it,
  not through the body's mechanics.  The s42 lineage stands nose-down from the start (held pelvis pitch -1.45 deg
  at the start, -1.35 at the ends, against -0.46 and -0.25 for s80) with its knee and hip angles set differently; its
  networks map that posture to a positive tail offset; the learned tail_3 positive feedback amplifies the offset to
  the bound.  Whether training built the pathway because of the posture, or the two grew together, is not testable
  without training [inferred].

4. LIMITS
==================================================================================================================
- Observation-only results describe the network's map near the settled states; transplanted values are medians,
  and big shifts (the neck, 4-9 SD) are out of distribution and not interpreted.  The linear fixed point ignores the
  map's curvature and the clip at +1.
- The closed-loop illusion is answered by the policies' own posture control (s42 ends pitched 0.47 deg further
  nose-down, s80 ends 0.84 deg nose-up), so the perceived change was smaller than the 1.1 deg applied, and actual
  and perceived pitch moved in opposite directions.
- Deterministic play only; one start checkpoint per lineage; 20 seeds; 10 s episodes (plus the 20 s check on 5
  seeds).  The ECS screens' stochastic training dynamics are not touched: whether the posture pathway causes the
  rail to form in training cannot be tested without training.
- Availability: everything these tests needed was local (checkpoints under r10round/ecs/runs, read only; the code
  copy under policies/code_copy; tailround's r9 method).  Nothing was fetched.
- Not done: the r9 lineage's posture pathway; tail_1/tail_2 pathways in closed loop; leg-angle illusions in closed
  loop (the legs' share rests on the observation-only transplant); stochastic (own-noise) play.

==================================================================================================================
0. IN SHORT
==================================================================================================================
- Mechanically the rail does almost nothing to the stance.  With the rail, tail_3 sits at +5.2 deg and the tail tip is
  about 65-70 mm higher.  The whole-body CoM moves 0.2 mm forward and 1.6 mm up, and the pelvis pitches 0.01 deg.  The
  numbers are the same in the s42 policies, the s80 policies and the neutral-action statue.  Two arms give them: the
  open-loop replay (the policy's own other 14 commands replayed exactly) and the statue.  So the rail is not a
  counterweight, as the static check found.  [measured: run_replay.py -> analysis.txt OPEN-LOOP and STATUE]
- The rail does cost reward directly: about 80-100 per 10 s episode.  In the open-loop replay it is -81 (s42), -97
  (s80) and -97.5 (statue).  The terms are action_saturation (-24 to -33), tail_home_pose (-55 to -62) and energy
  (-4 to -5).  [measured: analysis.txt]
- (i) Holding tail_3 at 0 on the 12 railed s42 end policies:
  - No falls.  No change in sway, CoM speed, lateral lean, roll or creep.
  - Return rises +63.6 per episode on 12 of 12 policies and 239 of 240 episodes.  D-9's window (2-10 s) gains +60.4.
  - The pelvis pitches 0.16 deg further nose-down (10 of 12, p 0.039).  The CoM drops 3.4 mm (10 of 12, p 0.039).
  - The fore-aft CoM moves +1.6 mm forward on the median (8 forward, 4 back, p 0.39).  Each policy is consistent over
    its 20 seeds, but the policies range from -6.1 to +11.6 mm.
  - So removing the rail does not move s42 toward s80's posture.  Pitch, height and fore-aft all move away from s80, or
    not at all.
  - It does not reverse the 5 mm backward drift at the ends: the lineage median goes from -25.7 to -24.9 mm, against
    -20.7 mm at the start.  [measured: run_clamp.py -> analysis.txt s42]
- (ii) Forcing tail_3 to +1 on the 12 s80 end policies:
  - No falls.  Return falls -180 per episode (12 of 12).
  - No consistent posture shift.  Pitch moves nose-up +0.45 deg (8 of 12, p 0.39).  Fore-aft moves +1.0 mm (7/5) and
    height -5.7 mm (5/7).
  - The spread across policies doubles to triples: fore-aft SD 4.9 -> 9.5 mm, pitch SD 0.32 -> 0.96 deg.
  - The lateral stance gets worse.  |CoM sideways| rises +0.58 mm (11 of 12, p 0.006) and |roll| +0.04 deg (12 of 12).
    The per-seed lateral SD goes from 1.75 to 4.05 mm (10 of 12).  In 20 s runs the sideways lean keeps growing:
    +3.7 mm at 15-20 s, 3 of 3 policies.
  - So imposing the rail does not move s80 "the same way".  The one shared direction is a nose-up pitch, and in s80 it
    is not consistent.  [measured: run_clamp.py -> analysis.txt s80, LONG RUNS]
- (iii) What the rail is, as far as these data show:
  - It is a closed loop inside the policy's own tail_3 channel.  When the s42 policies' tail is held at 0, their own
    tail_3 command settles at 0.35.  When the tail is free, the command climbs to 0.9 within about 2.5 s, as the tail
    rises.  The s80 policies push the other way: their own command is -0.27 when the tail is forced up.
  - What the rail changes in the stance, it changes through the policy, not the plant.  The s42 policies hold the head
    3.5 deg higher with the rail (head_pitch command -0.14 on 12 of 12).  They hold the pelvis 0.17 deg more nose-up and
    the CoM about 2 mm higher.
  - That coupling is opposite in sign to the s42-versus-s80 pitch gap: s42 is the more nose-down lineage.  So it does
    not explain the gap.
  [measured: rail_onset.txt, analysis.txt; loop interpretation inferred]

1. WHAT tail_3_pitch_act MEANS, AND WHAT D-9 OVERRODE
==================================================================================================================
[measured: probe_env.py -> probe_env.txt; code read in policies/code_copy]
- Action index 14 of 15 (the last), actuator tail_3_pitch_act on hinge tail_3_pitch.
  - It is a position servo: kp 60 N m/rad, force range +-50 N m.
  - ctrlrange is +-0.2094 rad (+-12 deg), with the joint range +-12 deg.
  - The joint has a preloaded spring: stiffness 80 N m/rad, springref +0.1815 rad (+10.4 deg).  This is physics r10's
    tail preload.
- Mapping, from tyrannosaurus_rex/base_env.py:
  - The action is clipped to [-1, 1].
  - It is then low-pass filtered: action_filter_cutoff_hz 10, alpha 0.386 per 10 ms step.  The filter is seeded with the
    first post-reset action (shared/base_env.py _filter_action).
  - _scale_action maps it as a residual about the home keyframe control, "home-keyframe-residual/v1".  tail_3's home
    ctrl is 0, so action 0 -> ctrl 0 rad and action +1 -> ctrl +0.2094 rad.
  - action_penalty_source = "raw": the saturation, jerk and smoothness terms price the pre-filter command; energy
    prices the applied one.
- The joint angle actually reached [measured: analysis.txt LEVELS]:
  - Command +1 reaches +5.19 deg, not +12 deg.
    - s80 forced: per-policy medians +5.17..+5.22.
    - s42 unforced: per-policy medians +4.42..+5.20.  s42_A_1151, whose window command averages 0.85, is the lowest.
      Single episodes go down to +2.0 deg where the command sits below the bound.
  - Command 0 reaches +0.014 deg.
  - The +1 command adds about 7.1 N m of servo torque (actuator force -7.13 when held at 0; +6.95 when forced).  Against
    gravity and the spring, that lifts the joint by about 5 deg.  [inferred from the parameters above and the measured
    angles]
  - + is tail tip up: +5.2 deg raises the tail tip 67.9 mm by forward kinematics [measured: probe_signs.py ->
    probe_signs.txt].  In the rollouts the tail tip is 63.5 mm (s42) and 72.0 mm (s80) higher.
- What D-9 overrode:
  - D-9 used the copy's episode_trace_report.py --hold tail_3_pitch_act=0.  Its reporting/episode_trace.py
    trace_episode takes action = model.predict(obs, deterministic=True)[0] and sets action[14] = 0 before env.step,
    every step.  So the policy acts on the true (held) state, and the env filters, scales and prices the held command.
  - run_clamp.py does exactly that: the policy's own action every step, with only entry 14 replaced.
- D-9 is reproduced [measured: d9_repro.py -> d9_repro.txt].  On D-9's own onset checkpoint (stance6 s42 3,670,016,
  the copy D-9 read, seeds 3042-3061):
  - Every seed's return and window return equal D-9's to within 9e-12.
  - The held joint angle matches to 6 digits.
  - The held-minus-baseline window return is +78.29 with 20 of 20 positive, D-9's number.

==================================================================================================================
8. (iii) WHAT THE RAIL IS DOING TO THE STANCE, AND WHAT THESE DATA CANNOT SHOW
==================================================================================================================
What they show:
1. Mechanics [measured: section 5]
   - The rail lifts the distal tail about 5 deg, so the tail tip rises 64-72 mm.
   - It moves the CoM 0.2 mm forward and 1.6 mm up, and the pelvis pitch by 0.01 deg.
   - That is negligible against the lineage differences (6.7 mm, 1.1 deg) and against the policies' own spawn-to-spawn
     spread (lateral SD 1.4-1.8 mm).  Not a counterweight.
2. Cost [measured: sections 3-5]
   - It costs about 80-97 reward per episode, directly in action_saturation, tail_home_pose and energy.
   - Removing it from the s42 ends gains +64 (closed loop) to +81 (open loop).
3. The rail is a self-reinforcing loop in the policy's tail_3 channel [measured: rail_onset.txt; loop interpretation
   inferred]
   - s42 unforced: the policy's own command goes from 0 at spawn to 0.53 at 0.75 s and 0.90 at 2.5 s, as the tail
     rises (+2.1 deg, then +4.6).
   - With the tail held at 0, the own command stays at 0.35 from 0.5 s on.
   - So the higher the tail, the more the s42 policy commands it up, until the bound.
   - The s80 policies have the opposite sign: with the tail forced up, their own command is -0.29.
4. The rail is tolerated, not needed [measured: sections 3 and 6]
   - Without it the s42 stance keeps the same lateral lean, roll, sway, creep and margins over 10 and 20 s.  None fall,
     and the return is higher.
   - What the s42 policies have learned to pair with the rail is a posture shift through the other actuators: the head
     held 3.5 deg higher (12/12), the pelvis about 0.17 deg more nose-up and the CoM about 2 mm higher (policy
     feedback).  The fore-aft CoM shifts by an amount and sign that depend on the policy.
5. The rail does not account for the s42-versus-s80 posture differences [measured: sections 3-4; inferred as stated]
   - Its pitch effect (nose-up) is opposite to s42's nose-down offset.

6. For a policy not trained with it (s80), the rail is a perturbation [measured: sections 4 and 6]

     -180, of which about -83 is the policies' own response.
   - The s42 policies' tolerance of the tail being up or down is therefore learned [inferred].

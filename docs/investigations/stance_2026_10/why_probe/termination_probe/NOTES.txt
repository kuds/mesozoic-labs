WHAT IT MEANS FOR "WHY BLIND THE CRITIC?"  [inferred]
- Blinding would be a bad fix if the critic needed the tail angle to foresee a real danger: tail_3/4/5 floor contact
  does end the episode with -100.  This lens finds no such danger in the era the habit grows.  The critic's tail
  preference is far larger than any termination risk could justify, and it does not even follow the tail's height.
  So blinding removed a spurious value gradient, not safety information.  BS2 is consistent with this: the climb
  stopped and returns did not fall, and here the blinded policy's lower tail ended no episode.
- Why it works mechanically: in PPO the critic does not act.  Its values enter each action's advantage through
  r + gamma V(s') - V(s), summed by GAE.  A critic whose V wrongly rises with the tail angle therefore pays the
  policy for raising the tail, and the policy gradient climbs.  A critic that cannot see the angle cannot pass that
  bias into the advantage.  Meanwhile the actor still sees the angle, and the real per-step costs of a raised tail
  (tail_home_pose, action_saturation) still reach the policy through the rewards.
- It treats the symptom, not the cause.  PH7 suggests the critic used the tail angle as a clock, because the reward
  changes with hidden episode time and the observation carries none.  A blinded critic can rebuild the shortcut from
  other slowly drifting inputs: tail_3's own velocity (obs 27) is still visible, and s80's habit was on the neck.  A
  root-cause fix would give the critic the missing information directly, for example the step index or a settled flag
  as a critic-only input (the actor's interface unchanged).  Another would make the stance terms not depend on hidden
  time.  Neither is tested here; lens A owns that question.

3. POWER AND FALSE-POSITIVE RATE [computed, POW, by exact enumeration unless marked]
======================================================================================================================
3.1 THE TEST
- Exact conditional test of a common odds ratio, stratified by start (exact Cochran-Mantel-Haenszel, "CMHX" in POW).
  T = the number of positive continuations in arm A, summed over starts. Its null distribution is the convolution of
  the per-start hypergeometric distributions (k, k, m_s), conditional on each start's total m_s. One-sided: B lower.
- With k per arm in every start, this is exactly the stratified permutation test (labels permuted within starts) on
  the summed A-minus-B difference, because within a start that difference is 2 a_s - m_s. So "exact CMH" and
  "stratified permutation test" are one test here.
- Pooling the starts into one Fisher test gives similar power at k = 3-4 and slightly more at k = 6 (POW section 3,
  bracketed). It ignores the starts, which differ, so the stratified test is the cleaner choice [inferred].

- k = 6: single starts become readable (4/6 against 0/6 gives 0.030; 5/6 against 1/6 gives 0.040; 0.889 at 0.9
  against 0.1).
3.5 THE NULL'S FALSE-POSITIVE RATE (POW section 6)
- The test is exact, so its size is at most 0.05 at every null rate. Actual sizes at a common rate p_s:
    k = 3: 0.0015-0.0203;  k = 4: 0.0022-0.0148;  k = 6: 0.0047-0.0240 (rates 0.1-1.0 tried).
  The largest over every pair of null rates on the grid 0.05, 0.10, ..., 0.95 is 0.0203 / 0.0152 / 0.0242.
  The sizes are smallest at null rates near 0 or 1, the region this screen expects for arm A.
- Paired seeds (seed j run in both arms, as common random numbers): with positively correlated pairs the test stays
  valid and grows more conservative (rho +0.5: 0.0002-0.0036). With negative correlation (rho -0.2) the size is up to
  0.0335, still under 0.05. The exact sign test on discordant pairs is a paired alternative; it is weaker at k = 3
  and about equal at k = 4-6 (POW 6b).
- Several readings or add-ons, each at 0.05: the chance that at least one reads p <= 0.05 by chance is at most the
  sum of their sizes (four readings at about 0.02 each: under 0.10). Hence one primary, the rest report only or at a
  split alpha.
- A two-sided reading (0.025 per side) costs little at k >= 4: 0.932 against 0.939 at 0.9 against 0.1; 0.700 against
  0.747 at 0.9 against 0.25 (POW 6c).

4. WHAT A NULL AND A POSITIVE WOULD AND WOULD NOT SHOW [inferred, from sections 1-3, REVIEW 4.2-4.4 and NEXT RISKS]
======================================================================================================================
These are mid-rise starts. At s42 3,145,728 tail_3 is already +0.524. At s80 3,145,728 the neck is at -0.382 (and
-0.594 at 4,194,304). Both bonuses are already at 55% (or 40%) of 0.005. The screen therefore tests whether the
REMAINING bonus affects the CROSSING of the bar from an already-rising state. It does not test the early rise
(0-3.1M steps), which every arm shares.

A POSITIVE (B lower at p <= 0.05) WOULD SHOW
- From these fixed training states, on CPU, with this harness, removing the remaining bonus (about 0.002 on average
  over the window) lowers the chance of an ANY-flagged evaluation within the window. It would be a causal effect at
  these states, since the arms differ only in ent_coef and start from the same pair.
- The bonus's gradient reaches log_std only (REVIEW 4.3, E7). So a positive would move the noise-level path from
  "not supported, not excluded" to supported from these states. The logged std (manipulation check, section 5.2)
  would show whether arm B's tail_3 or neck std fell faster.
A POSITIVE WOULD NOT SHOW
- That the bonus caused the rise, or the timing of the first onset from scratch (REVIEW 4.4), or that ent_coef 0
  from step 0 would prevent the habit. That is Option 3's question, and ent_coef 0 from scratch also changes early
  learning (NEXT Option 3 RISKS).
- A rate for from-scratch runs, a result on GPU, or one for other seeds' states.
- Prevention rather than delay, unless the first-flag step (secondary) shows B's onsets missing, not just later.
  The binary outcome reads "no" for any delay longer than the margin (655,360-917,504 steps).
- Anything about the round's reading, classes or gate, or a fix. Removing the bonus is not a stance recipe.
A NULL (p > 0.05) WOULD SHOW
- No large effect from these states within the window. At k = 4, an effect of 0.9 against 0.1 would have been seen
  with probability 0.94, so a null makes it unlikely. 0.9 against 0.5 would have been seen only 34% of the time.
A NULL WOULD NOT SHOW
- That the bonus plays no role in the early phase or the onset's timing (untested), or that ent_coef does not matter
  from scratch. A null is not evidence against Option 3 (NEXT l216-l218).
- A null "no effect" either, when arm A itself did not show the outcome. If arm A fires in few continuations at a
  start (say fewer than half), that start tested nothing. Section 6, D-6 proposes an "informative" check like
  D-D43's onset note (TSC criteria l264-l267).
Prior expectation [inferred, from REVIEW 4.3 and from D-D43's r9 CONTROL keeping the original's timing to within one
checkpoint from a +0.63 start]: from mid-rise states the crossing looks close to committed. The bonus's direct pull
is on log_std only. So a null is the more likely result, and the screen is mainly able to rule out a large effect.
An earlier start gives the bonus more leverage, at about 1.5x the cost from 2,097,152 and about 2x from 1,048,576
(POW section 8), with a less certain arm-A rate (section 1.3).

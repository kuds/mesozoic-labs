SHORT ANSWER
  No reward term pulls the r10 policies onto their toes.  The reward is the same function of posture on both plants up
  to lambda 0.5: on the held legs-only postures every spawn survives on both (lambda 0-0.5), the total-return slope is
  -109 (r10) and -109 (r9) per unit CoP per episode, and leg_home_pose is 497.6 / 497.4 at the statue and 456.3 /
  456.0 at lambda 0.5.  Further forward r10 charges more for the same posture, not less (return -33 per episode
  against r9 at lambda 0.875, 4 paired spawns).  What differs between the plants is the CoP a posture produces and
  whether it stands.
  (1) The r10 digits let a held lean stand further forward: the highest held lean most spawns survive is at CoP
      0.89-0.94 on r10 against 0.76-0.79 on r9 (single episodes 1.10 against 0.85).  On r10 nothing physical stops a
      forward drift before the 0.80 rail.  This permits the drift; it does not cause it.
  (2) What pushed all three r10 seeds forward early (CoP 0.18/0.33/0.32 at the first eval, 0.125M, against a held
      statue's 0.11) is not identified.  v1 said a forward lean pays under exploration noise on r10; that did not
      replicate on 20 new spawns and is withdrawn.  Under sigma 0.10 open-loop noise a modest lean (lambda 0.25) costs
      about nothing on r10 (+149 [-67,+381], 30 pairs) but about 807 on r9; at sigma 0.15 and 0.21 (the policies' std
      then was 0.218-0.219) it costs on r10 too.  The r10 statue falls backward (tail contact) more often than r9's at
      every sigma; most of its falls are backward at sigma 0.10 and 0.15 (3/4, 13/19), but not at sigma 0.21 (13/29),
      the noise closest to the policies' own std when the lean appeared.  In training, tail contact was 0.52-0.59 of
      r10's terminations in the first 131k steps against 0.30-0.38 on r9: a correlate, not a mechanism.
  Seed 42 made the same early excursion and came back, though its CoP is up again (0.32-0.42 in the last four evals:
  watch it).  Seeds 52 and 60 went on past the rail and stand at CoP 0.93 / 1.05 (4-6M): a closed-loop operating point
  at or beyond r10's open-loop edge (s52 at 0.930 is just under the diagnosis's 0.94 and past the verifier's 0.89; s60
  is past both), in a posture that does not stand open-loop (their own mean action DC held open-loop at lambda 1
  survives 0/8 with the legs alone and 2/8 with head and tail too), and in a basin that is worse on every material
  reward term.  What holds them there cannot be identified without their checkpoints.

5. ANSWERS TO THE QUESTIONS
  does any term pay more for the toes on r10: only marginally, and no more than on r9.  Along a legs-only lean
    (leg_dc) over whole-foot CoP 0.1-0.6, head_clearance (+1.1 / +1.1), nosedive (+5.1 / +4.0) and posture (+1.1 /
    +1.1) rise with the lean, together under +10 per unit CoP per episode, against a total-return slope of -122 (r10)
    and -138 (r9).  On r10, beyond 0.6, nosedive and posture turn negative.  There is no 'penalises less on r10'
    difference: the reward is the same function of posture on both plants up to lambda 0.5 (CoP about 0.5)
    (leg_home_pose 497.6 / 497.4 at the statue, 456.3 / 456.0 at lambda 0.5), and beyond lambda 0.5, r10 charges more
    for the same posture (paired on the same spawns, r10 minus r9 per episode: return -8 at lambda 0.625 to -33 at
    0.875 (4 pairs), there foot_flatness -8.3 and nosedive -7.4).  The per-unit-CoP gap (-119 against -139 over
    0.1-0.6) comes from r10 putting the same posture further forward (CoP 0.501 against 0.479 at lambda 0.5) and from
    which postures the CoP window keeps.  On identical postures (lambda 0-0.5) the total slopes are -109 and -109.
  is the slope steeper than on r9: no.  Per posture the reward is the same up to lambda 0.5, and beyond it r10 charges
    more; per unit CoP, legs only, the total slope is -176 (r10) and -182 (r9) over the shared range 0.1-0.85 (r9 has
    no survivors past it), and on the verifier's new spawns -159 and -157.  See probe.slopes_per_unit_cop_per_episode
    (seed fixed effects, seed-block bootstrap, 90% intervals).
  settled target or lean term: neither drives the drift.  The settled leg target supplies most of the restoring slope
    (-106 / -109 per unit on identical postures).  Centred on the keyframe instead, the term would pay for a forward
    lean: per posture it peaks at lambda 0.375 (494.5 / 494.2 per episode, against 470.1 / 469.7 at the statue and
    491.5 / 491.3 at lambda 0.5), equally on both plants; the +54 / +57 fit over lambda 0-0.5 averages across that
    peak.  The lateral lean term is small along a symmetric lean to CoP 0.6 (-3.5 / -0.3) and charges beyond (-15 /
    -24 over 0.6-1.15).
  does digit support let a forward lean survive: yes, further than on r9, by an amount that depends on the definition.
    The highest held lean most spawns survive (at least 5 of 8) is at CoP 0.89-0.94 on r10 against 0.76-0.79 on r9;
    held leans 7 of 8 survive reach 0.92 and 0.69; single episodes reach 1.10 (0.221 m) against 0.85 (0.201 m), and r9
    falls (nosedive) at every held lean past that.  So the 0.80 rail on r10 sits inside the region the plant can hold,
    while on r9 it lies at about the toppling line (0.196 m = pad 0.80).

6. WHAT THE DATA CANNOT TELL
  - why all three r10 seeds pushed forward in the first 0.125M steps.  v1's explanation (a forward lean pays under
    exploration noise on r10) did not replicate (H1b).  The remaining evidence is the in-training termination-share
    asymmetry, a correlate.  Testing it needs training-side runs (e.g. a lower initial log_std), which are out of
    scope
  - no checkpoint was replayed (analysis only; the policies stay on Drive).  What holds seeds 52 / 60 in their basin
    (H2) needs their policies, e.g. a closed-loop noise panel or a DC shift back toward seed 42's posture
  - the probe holds open-loop postures along one direction, the mean leg (and axial) action DC of seeds 52 / 60.  A
    closed-loop policy can hold postures and CoPs the probe cannot (seeds 52 / 60 stand at CoP 0.93 / 1.05, at or
    beyond the open-loop edge, and their own mean action DC held open-loop at lambda 1 survives 0/8 with the legs
    alone and 2/8 with head and tail too), and the probe's noise has no feedback.  'Edge' always means the open-loop
    edge
  - r9 training telemetry has the pad CoP only.  r9 whole-foot values are estimates from the probe's held-posture fit
    (whole-foot m = 0.1308 + 0.0824 x pad).  Reading an r9 pad CoP p directly as an r10-span whole-foot CoP is off by
    -0.075 + 0.077p units (estimate minus reading): -0.10 at r9 s52's 4-6M pad CoP (-0.33).  It is within 0.05 unit
    only for pad 0.32-1.62.  The pad CoP rows are each plant's own box (r10 [0.060, 0.165] m, r9 [0.070, 0.210] m) and
    are not comparable across plants without the metres row
  - three seeds per plant.  Why seed 42 escaped and seeds 52 / 60 did not cannot be attributed: the early excursion is
    common to all three, and in the divergence window (0.6-1.6M) the eval terms only follow the posture (seed 42:
    leg_home_pose up, neck_posture down as it raised its head; seeds 52 / 60: leg_home_pose down).  That says what
    changed, not why
  - the in-training termination record is the share of each reason among a rollout's terminations, not counts, so the
    early backward / forward asymmetry is a share, not a rate
  - why the r10 statue falls backward more often than r9's under noise is not isolated.  v1 cited 'a forward margin so
    much larger (toe margin 29.1 against 22.5 N m)'.  Those figures are quoted from
    docs/investigations/TREX_FOOT_R10_2026_10.md and measure each plant's statue against its own held-offset toe line
    (0.213 / 0.196 m).  Against the common 0.213 m line the probe's statues read 28.8 (r10) and 29.1 (r9) N m, so 'a
    larger forward margin' holds only measured to each plant's own toe line.  A rebound off the stiff MTP springs is
    the other candidate; neither is tested
  - harness: the r9 runs were trained locally with run_arm.py and the scratch screen_com_arm wrapper; the r10 runs on
    Colab with the notebook and the merged #624 options.  Env equivalence is shown (statue return 3981 / 3980; the env
    kwargs differ only by the arm L keys); training-side equivalence is not shown
  - the runs are still training (the stage's 12M budget).  The data stop at 5.75M (s42) and 5.88M (s52 / s60) of
    gate_progress

7. IMPLICATIONS
  For reading tomorrow's verdict:
  - K2 (fore-aft) will fail on seeds 52 and 60: their in-training evals are over the 0.80 rail on 10 of 10 episodes at
    every eval since 3.75M (s52) and 0.88M (s60).  The 40-episode panel is the same deterministic policy on other
    spawns.
  - K1 will very likely fail: max_foot_cop_fore_aft is a stance_quality/v2 clean criterion, so seeds 52 / 60 will be
    unclean on nearly every panel episode, and only seed 42 can pass (1 of 3 < 2).
  - read the failure as plant + training dynamics, not as a reward term paying for toes and not as an arm L defect:
    the reward is the same function of posture on both plants up to lambda 0.5 (CoP about 0.5), and beyond lambda 0.5,
    r10 charges more for the same posture (paired on the same spawns, r10 minus r9 per episode: return -8 at lambda
    0.625 to -33 at 0.875 (4 pairs), there foot_flatness -8.3 and nosedive -7.4); r10's wider forward margin lets a
    drift pass the rail; what started the drift early in training is not identified.
  - on r10 the 0.80 rail is a gate criterion with no physical backstop between 0.8 and about 0.9 (where most held
    leans still stand), whereas on r9 the rail lay at about the toppling line.  The rail did its job here.  Of the
    gate readings gate_progress records (the rail's over-bar fraction, the full-horizon fraction and the eval return),
    only the rail marks seeds 52 / 60: full horizon 0.988 / 0.994 / 0.971 (s42 / s52 / s60, 4-6M), eval return lower
    than seed 42's by 2.5% / 4.2%.  The other stance_quality/v2 episode criteria (sole tilt and corner lift, load
    shares, saturation, touchdowns and the rest) and K3's noise tests are not recorded there.  The training rollouts
    bear on K3's own-sigma test (at most 3 falls in 60 pooled): at 5-6M, under the policy's own noise, 28/115 (s52)
    and 55/121 (s60) rollouts of 8192 env steps had a termination, against 5/112 (s42).  They are not a K3 forecast:
    on r9 the same measure was 28/128, 6/128 and 33/128 (s42 / s52 / s60), and r9's own-sigma test at 6M had 0 falls
    in 60.
  - seed 42 is a clean-looking r10 stance (4-6M CoP 0.33, digits 41% of the load, toe margin 22 N m, rail over-bar
    fraction 0.03) and a candidate under the agreed pilot-start rule, against r9 seed 60 (40/40 clean at 6M).  Watch
    it: its CoP has been back up at 0.32-0.42 in the last four evals (since 2.4M 0.15-0.45, trend +0.06 per M after
    2M).  That is fine for the 6M verdict, but the runs train to 12M; check its CoP and over-bar fraction at the
    checkpoint actually used.
  For the walking pilot:
  - do not start from a drifted r10 checkpoint.  It stands at its toe line (4-6M toe margin 2.3 / -1.6 N m, digits 62
    / 72% of the load), so a forward step has no margin left.  The agreed rule (most clean panel episodes) already
    excludes them.
  - the pilot does not repeat the stance runs' early noise.  It warm-starts from a 6M stance checkpoint: train_base.py
    loads it with load_sb3_model, which calls SB3's alg_cls.load and so restores the parent's learned log_std (neither
    locomotion.toml's [ppo.policy_kwargs] nor the pilot's stage configs set log_std_init; the TOML itself records std

  - physics: r10's forward margin comes from the MTP springs (3000 / 4500 / 3000 N m/rad, the lowest rung of the

    stopped.  The leg-scale digits (0.157 / 0.194 / 0.163 m) or a softer rung would move the edge back toward the

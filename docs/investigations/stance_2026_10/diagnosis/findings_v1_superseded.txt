SHORT ANSWER
  No reward term pulls the r10 policies onto their toes.  Along a held forward lean the reward falls the same way
  on both plants: total return -192 (r10) and -196 (r9) per unit CoP per episode over 0.1-1.0.
  Two physical differences explain the drift instead.
  (1) Under exploration noise the r10 statue falls BACKWARD (tail contact), while r9 falls FORWARD (nosedive).
      So a forward leg lean pays under noise on r10 and costs on r9.  All three r10 seeds leaned forward within
      the first 0.25M steps; no r9 seed did.
  (2) The r10 digits keep a held forward lean standing out to CoP 1.06-1.10, against at most 0.85 on r9, so a
      forward-drifting r10 policy is not stopped by falls until it is past the 0.80 rail.
  Seed 42 made the same early excursion and came back.  Seeds 52 and 60 went on to r10's fall edge and stayed,
  in a basin that is worse on every reward term.  What holds them there cannot be identified without their checkpoints.

4. RANKED HYPOTHESES
  1. H1_noise_fall_asymmetry_plus_wider_forward_margin [best supported]
     No reward term pulls the r10 policies onto their toes.  Two physical differences do.  Under exploration noise, the r10 statue tends to fall BACKWARD (tail contact), while r9 falls FORWARD (nosedive).  A forward leg lean therefore raises the noisy return on r10 and lowers it on r9.  And the r10 digits move the survivable forward lean 0.21-0.26 units (16-20 mm) further toe-ward, past the 0.80 rail, so a forward-drifting r10 policy is not stopped by falls until about CoP 1.0.  On r9 the physics stopped it at about 0.8 (r9 seed 60 drifted to pad 0.86, whole-foot about 0.85, at 5.38M: r9's own edge).
     - against / limits: the probe's noise is open-loop (no feedback), harsher than a closed-loop policy's own noise; only the ordering across lean levels and plants is read, not absolute survival
     - against / limits: the noise optimum on r10 is a modest lean (lambda 0.25, deterministic CoP about 0.3, about 0.5 under the noise), not the 0.9-1.1 where seeds 52 / 60 sit: H1 explains the early forward excursion of all three r10 seeds and why nothing stops it, not why two seeds went on to the edge and stayed
  2. H2_trapped_in_a_worse_basin [consistent, mechanism not identified]
     Seeds 52 and 60 are in a worse posture basin, not a better one.  At 4-6M they earn less than seed 42 on deterministic evals and on stochastic training rollouts, and they terminate more often.  Seed 42 made the same early excursion (CoP about 0.6 at 0.4-1.0M) and came back between 1.0M and 1.6M.  Seeds 52 and 60 kept going until they reached r10's fall edge.
     - against / limits: the open-loop escape path (keep the raised head and tail, bring the legs back) shows no valley on r10: return and survival rise steadily all the way back, so what holds the policies is not visible in held postures.  The likely candidate is that a policy's balance feedback is tuned to its own operating point.  Testing that needs the checkpoints.
     - against / limits: seed 60 is creeping back slowly (CoP 1.19 at 2M -> 1.07 at 6M); seed 52 is not (0.92 -> 0.96)
  3. H3_a_reward_term_pays_for_the_toes [rejected as a driver (only small positive terms)]
     A reward term pays more for standing further onto the toes.
  4. H4_settled_leg_target [not a cause; it is most of the restoring force]
     The settled leg target of arm L drives the drift.
  5. H5_lateral_lean_term [no role]
     The lateral lean term pays for the fore-aft drift.

5. ANSWERS TO THE QUESTIONS
  does any term pay more for the toes on r10: only marginally.  Over whole-foot CoP 0.1-0.6, head_clearance, nosedive and posture rise with the lean on both plants, together under +10 per unit CoP per episode, against a total-return slope of -130 (r10) and -143 (r9).  On r10, beyond 0.6, nosedive and posture turn negative.  The only material 'penalises less' difference is leg_home_pose: -122 on r10 against -138 on r9 per unit (0.1-0.6).  Over 0.1-1.0 the total slope is the same within its interval on both plants: -192 and -196.
  is the slope steeper than on r9: no material difference.  See slopes_per_unit_cop_per_episode (seed-block bootstrap, 90% intervals).
  settled target or lean term: neither drives the drift.  The settled leg target supplies most of the restoring slope and removes the forward pull that the keyframe target would have.  The lateral lean term is flat along a symmetric lean to CoP 0.6 and charges a little beyond that.
  does digit support let a forward lean survive: yes.  A held symmetric lean survives open-loop to CoP 1.06 (level mean of survivors; single episodes to 1.10, 0.221 m) on r10, against at most 0.85 (0.201 m) on r9, which falls (nosedive) everywhere past that.  Held leans that 7 of 8 spawns survive reach CoP 0.92 on r10 and 0.69 on r9.  So the 0.80 rail on r10 sits inside the region the plant can hold, while on r9 the rail coincided with the toppling line (0.196 m = pad 0.80).

6. WHAT THE DATA CANNOT TELL
  - no checkpoint was replayed (analysis only; the policies stay on Drive).  What holds seeds 52 / 60 in their basin (H2) needs their policies, e.g. a closed-loop noise panel or a DC shift back toward seed 42's posture
  - the probe holds open-loop postures along one direction, the mean leg (and axial) action DC of seeds 52 / 60.  A closed-loop policy can hold postures and CoPs the probe cannot, and the probe's noise has no feedback
  - r9 training telemetry has the pad CoP only.  r9 whole-foot values are estimates from the probe's held-posture fit (whole-foot m = 0.1308 + 0.0824 x pad).  The r9 pad span [0.070, 0.210] and the r10 whole-foot span [0.060, 0.213] make the normalised readings roughly comparable (within about 0.05 unit over the observed range), not identical
  - three seeds per plant.  Why seed 42 escaped and seeds 52 / 60 did not cannot be attributed: the early excursion is common to all three, and in the divergence window (0.6-1.6M) the eval terms only follow the posture (seed 42: leg_home_pose up, neck_posture down as it raised its head; seeds 52 / 60: leg_home_pose down).  That says what changed, not why
  - the in-training termination record is the share of each reason among a rollout's terminations, not counts, so the early backward / forward asymmetry is a share, not a rate
  - why the r10 statue falls backward under noise is not isolated.  Candidates are a rebound off the stiff MTP springs, or a forward margin so much larger (toe margin 29.1 against 22.5 N m) that the backward falls remain.  The probe only shows that it does
  - the runs are still training.  The data stop at 6.03M (s42) and 6.16M (s52 / s60) of gate_progress

7. IMPLICATIONS
  For reading tomorrow's verdict:
  - K2 (fore-aft) will fail on seeds 52 and 60: their in-training evals are over the 0.80 rail on 10 of 10 episodes at every eval since 3.75M (s52) and 0.88M (s60).  The 40-episode panel is the same deterministic policy on other spawns.
  - K1 will very likely fail: max_foot_cop_fore_aft is a stance_quality/v2 clean criterion, so seeds 52 / 60 will be unclean on nearly every panel episode, and only seed 42 can pass (1 of 3 < 2).
  - read the failure as plant + training dynamics (r10's wider forward margin and backward-falling statue under exploration noise), not as a reward term paying for toes and not as an arm L defect.  The deterministic reward landscape along the lean is the same on both plants.
  - on r10 the 0.80 rail is a gate criterion with no physical backstop between 0.8 and about 1.0, where on r9 the rail coincided with the toppling line.  The rail did its job here: it is the only thing that marks seeds 52 / 60.
  - seed 42 is a clean-looking r10 stance (4-6M CoP 0.33, digits 41% of the load, toe margin 22 N m, rail over-bar fraction 0.03) and a candidate under the agreed pilot-start rule, against r9 seed 60 (40/40 clean at 6M).
  For the walking pilot:
  - do not start from a drifted r10 checkpoint.  It stands at its toe line (4-6M toe margin 2.3 / -1.6 N m, digits 62 / 72% of the load), so a forward step has no margin left.  The agreed rule (most clean panel episodes) already excludes them.
  - the walking stage's own early training uses the same initial exploration noise (log_std_init -1.5, sigma about 0.22).  On r10 that noise favours a forward lean near stance-like states, and a forward-velocity reward will add to it.  Watch the whole-foot CoP, digit share and toe margin in the pilot's gate_progress from the first evals: a drift toward 1.0 would be this mechanism.
  - if the pilot starts from an r9 checkpoint, it inherits r9's physics backstop at 0.196 m.

8. OPTIONS FOR THE MAINTAINER (descriptions only)
  - these are descriptions, not plans: under the agreed policy no new stance-only reward experiment is proposed
  - physics: r10's forward margin comes from the MTP springs (3000-4500 N m/rad, the lowest rung that beat r9's push limit) and the top-of-range digit lengths.  Either sets where a forward drift is stopped.  The leg-scale digits (0.157 / 0.194 / 0.163) or a softer rung would move the edge back toward the rail, at a cost in forward push limit (note 20)
  - gate (no change involved): the whole-foot rail at 0.80, as set, is what separates seeds 52 / 60 from seed 42 here; full horizon and the return bar barely do (eval return differs by 2.5-4%)
  - exploration: the early forward push comes from falls under the initial noise.  A lower initial log_std would change it, but that is a training change that only a new run could test
  - reward: a fore-aft CoM / CoP price (arm C's term on r9) would act on the drift directly.  It exists in the r9 harness only and is not proposed here

2. THE COMPSOGNATHUS ROBOT CREEP (cause found; verified with corrections; notes/creep, notes/verify_creep)
======================================================================================================
What it is [measured, verified]: a simulation artefact, not physics. The ankle-roll servo's damping acts on a very
light joint (armature an assumed 1e-4). MuJoCo steps that damping implicitly (through M + h*D) while the contact
solver predicts each step with the plain M. On a spawn that lands pre-stressed on its sole edges, the mismatch lets
the contacts do net positive work (+7.7 mJ per cycle), which drives an exact 25 Hz rock-and-hop; each foot leaves the
floor once per cycle and the robot slides back 3-4 mm/s. The servo commands are constant: the 50 Hz control rate
plays no part (#650's "aliased" reading was wrong). The same happens with implicit joint damping under Euler.
Corrected by the verifier: the lens's simple criterion (a damping eigenvalue above 2) is refuted. At the same
eigenvalue, more armature or a 1 ms step removes the creep, so it is not a usable plant check.
The fix [measured, verified]: robot armature 1e-4 -> 3e-4 (robot physics r1 -> r2). It stops all 10 known creepers,
0 of 200 new seeds creep (against 6 of 200 today), resting spawns keep their posture within 0.003 mm and 0.002 deg,
and it holds even at 2-3x the servo damping. But:
  - It is a plant change: every robot checkpoint is invalidated and the robot's recovery calibration must be redone.
  - As drafted it leaves 9 shared tests failing (3 robot dynamics goldens to recapture, 6 tied to the recovery
    calibration), and its notes state the refuted criterion; both need fixing before it could land.
  - The robot trains and evaluates at reset noise 0.01, where 0 of 202 spawns creep (including the 10 known
    creepers). The creep shows only at the CoM tests' noise 0.02. So it does not touch training today.
Also found: the anatomical Compsognathus's ankle and the Velociraptor's claw have similarly light, heavily damped
joints; whether they creep is untested (the eigenvalue cannot say). The robot README's note that damping was halved
"to remove transient rocking" was plausibly this same artefact [inferred].

4. THE TAIL CLAMP TEST (notes/clamp, notes/verify_clamp)
======================================================================================================
Exploratory and report-only (not pre-registered). The 24 entropy-screen end policies, deterministic, seeds 3042-3061,
1,000 steps (and 2,000 on 3 policies per arm): the 12 s42 (railed) policies unforced and with tail_3 held at 0; the 12
s80 (no rail) policies unforced and with tail_3 forced to +1. The override is D-9's (only action entry 14 replaced) and
reproduces D-9 exactly; every unforced episode is bit-identical to the policies lens's; no falls in any arm. The
verifier re-ran a subset with its own loop (bit-identical) and checked the override inside the env at every step.
What the rail is mechanically [measured, verified]:
  - Command +1 holds the tail tip up only +5.2 deg, not 12: the servo and the tail's preloaded spring split the
    stiffness (60 against 80), and the preload cancels gravity at 0.
  - On the plant alone (open-loop replay of each episode's own commands, and the statue) it moves the CoM 0.2 mm
    forward and 1.6 mm up and the pelvis 0.01 deg: the same in every policy. It is not a counterweight.
  - It costs about 80-100 reward per episode directly (action saturation, tail home pose, energy).
(i) Removing it from s42 [measured, verified]: return +63.6 per episode (12 of 12 policies). The stance is otherwise
  unchanged: lateral lean, roll, sway, creep and margins the same over 20 s. The pelvis goes 0.16 deg further nose-down
  and the CoM 3.4 mm lower (10 of 12), and every policy lowers its head 3.5 deg (12 of 12). The rail is tolerated, not
  needed. Removing it moves s42 away from s80's posture, not toward it, and does not undo the lineage's 5 mm drift.
(ii) Imposing it on s80 [measured, verified with corrections]: return -180 (12 of 12; about half direct, half the
  policies' reaction). No shared posture shift; instead large, policy-specific neck re-postures (five policies lose
  head-clearance reward, two by 312-332), a worse sideways stance (11-12 of 12), and on some policies a lean that keeps
  growing (s80_B_1411 from 14 to 20 mm between 5 and 20 s). A body not trained with the rail is destabilised by it.
The mechanism [measured by the verifier's observation-only tail-angle sweep]: in the s42 policies the tail_3 command
  rises with the tail angle they observe (12 of 12), loop gain about 0.63 (0.48-0.91). That is a positive-feedback
  loop below 1, so it settles at a fixed point, but that point lies past the +1 bound: the command parks at the rail.
  Within an episode the command climbs from 0 to 0.9 in 2.5 s as the tail rises; with the tail held at 0 it stays at
  0.35. The continuation roughly doubled the gain (start checkpoint 0.27 -> ends 0.63). The s80 policies have the
  opposite sign (10 of 12 push back). The head-up coupling is the same kind of direct mapping from the observed tail
  angle (12 of 12).
The 5 mm backward drift of the railed lineage: it exists only in the tail-up state. With the tail held at 0 in both the
  start and the ends the drift is -0.7 mm; with both railed it is -7.7 mm. The policies' response to the rail reversed
  during the continuation. One start checkpoint; not shown to be caused by the rail in training.
What it means for the CoM question [inferred]: the rail is not a balance or CoM strategy, and the CoM tests cannot see
  it on the s42 policies, because their stance is the same with or without it. The actuator-saturation ledger stays the
  right detector. What the clamp adds is the mechanism: a learned positive feedback from the observed tail angle to the
  tail command, whose gain grew in training. That fits the round review's finding that the observation dimension that
  drifts most in normalisation is the joint that later saturates [inferred link, untested]: a shrinking normaliser std
  on the tail angle would raise exactly this gain.
Not shown: why the loop formed in training or what training without it would give; one start checkpoint per lineage;
  deterministic rollouts at one reset noise only; no tail_1/tail_2 clamps. Many keys were read unplanned, so isolated p
  near 0.04 are suggestive only.
Disclosed slip: one check wrote 68 .pyc files into the policies lens's code copy; deleted, no source changed (verified).

    read-back better than about 5 % of servo range.

    79 kept per push run in 15 s.
